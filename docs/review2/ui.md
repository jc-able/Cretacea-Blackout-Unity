# Review 2 (Nov 20, after round 2) — segment `ui` (score 7/10)

The UI is now one clear, readable system. It uses real fonts and a warm amber accent, the key prompts match on every screen, and the objective text tells a new player what to do and where. The quiet HUD and the typed document page come close to AAA. It is not yet amazing for two reasons. On the tablet map, labels collide with the YOU marker and the objective ring, and the destination's own label is the hardest one to read. On the end screen, the sun shows as a flat grey disc under a neutral overlay. Everything else is polish: a Courier transcript with a broken hanging indent, spoilers in the document list, an empty Systems page, unclear LIGHT ON/OFF wording, and a style with nothing that places it in 1996.

Strengths:
- One UI language everywhere: warm near-black cards, thin warm borders, amber for kickers, selection and values, cream body text. The HUD, pause, settings, tablet, document reader and end screen all match.
- Real fonts with clear roles, rendered crisply with soft shadows: Barlow Condensed for headings and labels, Barlow for body text, Courier Prime for typed paper, Stardos Stencil for the wordmark.
- The HUD is restrained. The objective banner is small and fades out (HudController), the dot reticle has a dark outline so it reads over the bright lamp in 02 and the dark corridor, and the flashlight chip hides itself.
- Objective copy is specific: verb, object and place. Example: 'Find the 1996 evacuation manual in the Records Annex, east end of the Staff Corridor'. The tablet backs it up with a map beacon and an amber place tag.
- Key prompts are consistent (TAB, ESC, E, F keycaps) on the HUD, the pause card, the tablet footer and the reader button.
- The document page is the best piece. It has typewriter ink, stains, a red letterhead, a boxed warning, a CONTROLLED COPY stamp and a reinforced binder hole, and the manual itself teaches the route.
- Subtitles carry an amber speaker tag on a dark plate and stay readable over the lit floor (about 10:1 mean contrast).
- Settings are laid out in two tidy columns whose rows line up. They include subtitles, sound captions and rumble toggles, plus a plain-language brightness note.
- No text is clipped or overflowing in any of the eleven frames. I checked the tablet's right column and route card at 5-6x zoom and every glyph is whole.

## Findings

- **[major] journal_tab0.png** (ui): Tablet map, top half. The YOU marker and its white direction arrow sit on the first letters of 'STAFF CORRIDOR', so it reads 'AFF CORRIDOR'. The pulsing objective ring covers the X of 'ANNEX'. 'STORAGE' touches both walls of its room and 'VET' is jammed against the Loading Dock wall. The destination label 'RECORDS ANNEX' is grey ink on the amber highlight at about 3:1, the least readable label on the map even though it is the one the player is looking for. These collisions sit exactly where the eye goes: YOU and the target.
  - Likely cause: ParkMapView.cs places every label at a fixed world point and never checks it against the moving player marker or the beacon. The corridor label is Label("STAFF CORRIDOR", new Vector2(-32f, 20.5f), 18f, InkFaint) at about line 311; the beacon is a 40 ref px ring at the objective point, about line 419. The fit loop stops shrinking at 16 px (while (t.fontSize > 16f && ...)), so names wider than an 8 m room overflow. The highlighted room keeps the InkMuted label colour.
  - Suggested fix: Each time the map is shown or the player moves, fade any label whose rect overlaps the player marker or the beacon to about 25% alpha, or slide the corridor label to the free end of the corridor. Anchor the target room's label to the top of the room and keep the beacon in the lower half. Draw the highlighted room's label in near-black ink (about #1A140C) or TextPrimary so it holds at least 4.5:1 on the amber. For rooms narrower than their name, allow 14 px, an abbreviation or a leader line (STORAGE, VET). Give the YOU marker a 1-2 px dark outline.
- **[major] 29_end_screen.png** (lighting): End screen, on the horizon right of centre. The sun is a flat neutral-grey disc (every pixel 194,194,194) crossed by a lemon stripe (194,194,40-80) inside a lemon halo. It looks like a matte sticker, duller than the clouds it is supposed to light. This is the game's payoff image, and its light source should be the brightest, warmest thing in the frame.
  - Likely cause: The DawnCoast key light (AtmosphereController.GetKeyLook, default case: 115000 lux, 5750 K, filter white, 4.5 degrees) clips the disc to 1.0 in all channels and the glow to R=G=1.0. That is lemon, against the code's own comment 'peach, not lemon'. MenuController.BuildEndingOverlay then lays CreateBackdrop(root, "EndingRoot", 0.46f), a neutral near-black plate, over it. Blended in linear, clipped white x 0.54 gives exactly sRGB 194 grey.
  - Suggested fix: Stop the clip at the source: give the DawnCoast key a warmer, dimmer disc (HDAdditionalLightData.surfaceTint about (1.0, 0.6, 0.38), lower flareMultiplier, peach flareTint), or lower its colour temperature toward 3500 K. On the end screen, replace the flat 0.46 plate with a warm vertical gradient (about 0.55 at top and bottom, about 0.2 across the horizon band), or dim only behind the stats card, so the sun and its glint on the sea keep their glow.
- **[minor] document_reader.png** (ui): Document reader, right column (same in 11_ch2_manual_open.png). The transcript repeats the page in Courier Prime, which is harder to read than the paper beside it. Its hanging indent is one character off: first lines start at x about 726, wrapped lines at x about 719. The numbered items have no space between them, so the version meant to be easier is denser than the original.
  - Likely cause: MenuController.BuildDocumentModal: _docTranscriptText uses UiFont.Mono, auto-size 18-22, lineSpacing 8 and no paragraph spacing. The indent comes from spaces in the source text.
  - Suggested fix: Set the transcript in UiFont.Body (Barlow, 24) with paragraphSpacing about 12 and a TMP <indent> (or <margin-left>) per numbered item for a true hanging indent. Alternatively, give the paper the full screen and put the transcript behind a [T] toggle, as RE7 and TLOU do.
- **[minor] journal_tab2.png** (story): Documents tab in chapter 2, nothing found yet. All five entries already show their exact future locations, including 'Tyrant Valley, at the overturned ranger jeep' and 'Old Maintenance Yard, in the foreman's office'. The map tab's route card also lists 'MASTER KEY: NOT FOUND' before the story has mentioned a key. The code deliberately hides Substation 3 and E-0 until the manual is read, yet this list gives away the route and the T. rex attack anyway.
  - Likely cause: JournalController.cs fills each row's hint from ArchiveDocs (for example line 122) whatever the player's progress. The master key row in the route status update (about line 836) is always shown.
  - Suggested fix: Show unfound entries as 'UNKNOWN DOCUMENT' and reveal the hint only once the player has entered that zone, or only for the current chapter. Hide the master key row until the key has been mentioned.
- **[minor] 02_ch1_flashlight_in_hand.png** (ui): Flashlight chip, bottom left. '[F] LIGHT ON' pairs a key with a state, so it reads like an instruction ('press F: light on') while the light is already on, and 'LIGHT OFF' reads the same way in reverse. The capture notes expected a key hint at the top right; there is none in this frame. The pickup teaches the key only through this chip's pulse, and the chip fades after 8 s.
  - Likely cause: HudController.RefreshFlashlightChipText writes 'LIGHT ON'/'LIGHT OFF' next to the keycap. OnFlashlightPickedUp suppresses the '[F] Toggle flashlight' toast.
  - Suggested fix: Name the action and show the state with the icon: '[F] FLASHLIGHT' with a lit or unlit beam icon (amber vs grey). Or show '[F] TOGGLE LIGHT' on the first pickup only, and afterwards just the state dot.
- **[minor] 06_ch1_blackout_talon_breakout.png** (ui): Radio subtitle, bottom centre. The first line runs about 85 characters across about 640 px and leaves 'now!' alone on the second line. Long lines are slow to read in a panic beat, and the orphaned word looks careless.
  - Likely cause: HudController: SubtitleMaxW = 1060 ref px (about 55% of the screen) at 28 px Barlow Medium, with no line balancing.
  - Suggested fix: Cap the subtitle body at about 860 ref px (roughly 45-55 characters per line). Split two-line captions at the word nearest the middle so both lines are similar in length. Keep the amber speaker tag.
- **[minor] hud_corridor.png** (story): The objective copy is specific but steers by compass: 'east end of the Staff Corridor' here, 'south end of the Lobby' in 06. The HUD has no compass, and in a dark corridor a new player cannot tell east from west without opening the tablet.
  - Likely cause: n/a
  - Suggested fix: Lead with a landmark the player can see, for example '...in the Records Annex, at the far end of this corridor past the Control Room'. Keep the compass word as a second clue, or show a small heading tick in the banner while it is visible.
- **[minor] journal_tab1.png** (ui): Before the manual is read, the SYSTEMS tab is a large empty brown box (about 980 x 440 px) holding a smaller black box that says 'NO DATA' and repeats the objective. It is a dead page in the middle of the tablet.
  - Likely cause: Empty state in JournalController.BuildSystemsChecklistPage.
  - Suggested fix: Show the systems diagram greyed out and locked (substation, circuits and gate as dim outlines, with 'NO SCHEMATIC: find the evacuation manual'), or hide the tab until the manual is read.
- **[minor] settings.png** (ui): Brightness row. The value reads '0', but the amber fill runs from the left end to the middle, which says 'half'. The slider is bipolar, and there is no reference image to set it against.
  - Likely cause: MenuController line 711: CreateSliderRow(leftCol, "Brightness", -1.5f, 1.5f, ...) uses the standard left-anchored fill.
  - Suggested fix: Fill from a centre tick to the knob (or show no fill, only a centre tick) and display '+0.0 EV'. Add the usual horror calibration panel on first launch: a dim logo that should be only just visible.
- **[minor] pause.png** (ui): Small mismatches between menus. The chapter line is grey Courier caps in the pause card ('CHAPTER 2 — RECORDS ANNEX') but amber Barlow Condensed in the tablet. Settings is the only screen without a key-hint footer: pause shows 'ESC RESUME' and the tablet shows 'TAB ESC CLOSE TABLET'. The three-letter TAB and ESC keycap glyphs are only about 6-7 px tall at 720p.
  - Likely cause: n/a
  - Suggested fix: Use the tablet's amber Barlow Condensed chapter style in the pause card and keep Courier for in-world paper. Add 'ESC BACK' to the settings footer. Widen multi-letter keycaps (CretaceaUiFactory.Keycap.SetKey) so the glyph stays at about 60% of the chip height instead of shrinking.
- **[minor] 29_end_screen.png** (ui): The victory card reads like a stats form: 'YOU ESCAPED CRETACEA PARK' at a modest size, three tiles, then a developer-style credit block crammed into the same card ('Built in Unity 6 HDRP for Isla Tormenta.' plus the font licence list). The last beat of a horror game carries no emotional weight.
  - Likely cause: Credit lines in GameState.BuildCompletionStatsSummary; layout in MenuController.BuildEndingOverlay.
  - Suggested fix: Hold the dawn shot clean for a few seconds. Then bring in a bigger headline (or the last radio line) and the three stats, and move the credits to a separate roll or a Credits button. Reword the first credit line, for example 'Made with Unity 6 HDRP'.
- **[minor] pause.png** (ui): The menus are clean but generic: flat near-black cards with hairline borders, no texture and no sense of period. The pause card, settings and tablet look like a modern app rather than a 1996 park-security device. The paper document and the end-screen badge show what this UI could be.
  - Likely cause: n/a
  - Suggested fix: Carry the park's identity into the menus: the Cretacea Park badge or stencil wordmark in the pause and tablet headers, faint paper or CRT grain in the panels, the tablet framed as a period device (a rugged PDA bezel with screen glow, or a binder for documents), and the stencil face for chapter titles.
