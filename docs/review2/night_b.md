# Review 2 (Nov 20, after round 2) — segment `night_b` (score 5/10)

Not amazing yet. The night route through the substation, green route, yard, E-0 and coast road now reads as a coherent, playable storm night: one lamp language, readable silhouettes, convincing wet asphalt, no crushed blacks and no banding. That is a solid AA look. Four things keep it from AAA: lightning whites out the yard while the sky stays black, chain-link everywhere (including the climax gate) reads as frosted glass, the flood at the aux-power payoff is blown out, and the coast road shows no sea. The ending is not beautiful yet: the first shot has a clipped cream sky, a lemon-yellow sun glow and a jagged sun disc, and the last two shots turn sepia, with a brown jungle and an olive sea.

Strengths:
- One lamp language holds across the climax: warm sodium and tungsten lamps (substation gate, generator-pad lamp, workshop wall lamp, coast-road cobra heads), a cool-white flood, green for the escape (bollards, E-0 sign and its downlight), an amber service bollard, and red only as small beacons and the flare.
- Night is playable: trees, pylons and buildings stand out as silhouettes against a blue-grey storm sky. No route frame has more than 2 % of its pixels at or below 8/255, and no sky shows banding (the grain dithers the gradients).
- Wet asphalt sells the storm: long lamp streaks, flashlight reflections, cracks and lane paint (23, 24, 26, 27).
- The route reads: the green bollard line in 22; the E-0 road sign in 25/26, with crisp lettering and a real light fitting; and the yard in 24, which leads the eye to the green sign on the far wall.
- Believable hardware: real transformer models, a breaker cabinet with breakers and status LEDs, a transfer box with a gauge and hazard stripe, textured pallets and crates.
- Rain reads as thin streaks lit by the lamps, and the flashlight throws a visible shaft through it (22).
- The UI is clean and consistent: objective panel, captions on a dark backing, letterbox bars.
- The ending has good bones: the rescue boat, the sun's glitter path in 28_ending_3, and low golden light with long shadows over the rain-wet yard in 28_ending_2.

## Findings

- **[blocker] NightStormBlackout_yard_eye.png** (weather): The yard caught mid lightning strike. The stacked containers on the right turn into glowing white boxes: the green container face is 181/180/170 here against 19/20/19 in play frame 24 from almost the same spot, about 9x brighter. The pallets and the concrete pad under the drum go white, and the wet asphalt on the left becomes white glitter. Meanwhile the sky stays near-black (31/34/41) with no lit clouds. The ground flashes and the sky does not, so it reads as a broken exposure, and it happens every 6.5-16 s outdoors in chapters 2-6.
  - Likely cause: AtmosphereController.GetKeyLook(NightStormBlackout) sets lightningLux = 190 (its own comment says 320 'whited out light concrete'). At storm EV 3.0-4.7 that puts a 0.3-0.5 albedo surface facing the strike at roughly 0.8-2.5x white (pixel = lux x albedo / pi / (1.2 x 2^EV)). The flash light has interactsWithSky = false, and nothing brightens the sky dome or the volumetric clouds; only the bolt mesh and the puddle decals read _LightningFlashIntensity. The glitter is specular aliasing on the soaked slab clones (WetnessDriver slabSmoothnessMax 0.94) under a hard light. presetshots.sh leaves lightning on, so this is what a player sees during a strike.
  - Suggested fix: Lower the peak to 50-90 lux so light concrete tops out near 0.8 linear, and keep the flash cool. Make the sky carry the flash: pulse the volumetric clouds' ambient/sun dimmer or the sky exposure with _lightningLevel, or add a sky-only light with interactsWithSky on, so the cloud deck glows and the world shows as silhouettes that keep their texture. Add geometric specular AA to the wet clones, or cap slab smoothness around 0.88.
- **[major] 28_ending_3.png** (colour): The ending goes sepia. This final shot averages 97/72/39. The upper sky is warm grey (96/77/64) where the bible asks for pale blue, the backlit clouds are mud-brown, and the sea is olive-brown (70/50/31) instead of teal. In 28_ending_2 the jungle canopy averages 41/28/10, which is brown, not green, and every shadow is brown. There is no cool skylight anywhere, so the warm sun has nothing to play against. (This frame peaks at 229, so a 10 % fade has started, but a fade does not change the hue.)
  - Likely cause: LookAtmosphereProfiles dawn: tint +3 (magenta, which drains the greens and turns the clouds pink-brown), temperature +1, smhHighlights (1.06, 1.0, 0.95), saturation +10. On top of that, fogAlbedo (0.92, 0.90, 0.90) is lit by a 4.5-degree orange sun with fogMeanFreePath 850 m and fogMaxHeight 42 m. The whole sea therefore sits in warm haze that hides the water's teal (LookTerrainWater refraction 0.10/0.55/0.55).
  - Suggested fix: Set tint to 0 and fix the lemon sun through its flare instead (see the next finding). Temperature -3 to -5, smhShadows toward blue (about 0.92, 0.98, 1.12), neutral highlights, saturation 0 to +4. Thin the dawn fog over the sea (mean free path about 3000 m, or max height about 15 m). Targets: canopy with green above red, a steel-teal sea with a bright reflected band at the horizon, a blue-grey zenith.
- **[major] 28_ending_1.png** (exposure): In the first ending shot the sky between the clouds is a flat cream with no gradation: 59 % of the sky area has red at 250 or above. The clouds are saturated pink-red, and the glow round the sun is neon lemon (sampled 255/254/34 and 255/231/1). The sea under that blazing sky stays a dull mauve-grey (115/94/92), with no bright band mirrored at the horizon. It reads as a postcard filter, nothing like the bible's peach-to-pale-blue sky.
  - Likely cause: Most likely the shot plays mid-blend. StoryDirector starts the 16 s crane shot with SetPreset(DawnCoast, 8.5f), so this shot runs while the exposure range and grade are still crossing over from the night look (BlendRoutine's lookK follows the sun's height) and the sun is still rising through the horizon. The Custom tone curve works per channel, so a bright saturated orange clips red, then green, and turns lemon. The key light's sun flare (size and tint) is left at HDRP defaults in AtmosphereController.
  - Suggested fix: Fade in from black on an already settled DawnCoast (SetPreset(DawnCoast, 0) with the exposure history reset), then move the camera. Give the sun a smaller peach flare (flareSize about 0.8, flareTint soft peach, surfaceTint off-white) so it rolls off to cream, not lemon. Target under 5 % clipped pixels outside the sun disc.
- **[major] 28_ending_1.png** (lighting): The sun, the focal point of the ending, renders broken. In 28_ending_1 it is a jagged, stair-stepped white sliver of about 30x8 px lying on the horizon, not a disc rising from the sea. In 28_ending_3 a hard, stair-stepped dark band slices the disc in two.
  - Likely cause: Low-resolution volumetric cloud edges (dawn clouds: Sparse, density 0.36, bottom 1500 m) cross a very bright disc and are upscaled and reprojected over time while the cutscene camera glides. The sun, at azimuth 104 degrees, sits inside the cloud field.
  - Suggested fix: Keep the sun in clear sky for the cutscene: shift the dawn cloud field with windOrientation or an offset, or thin it around the sun's azimuth. Turn on the clouds' ghosting reduction, perceptual blending and full-resolution clouds for the ending. Start the sun 1-2 degrees higher so it is a whole disc in the first shot.
- **[major] 25_ch6_e0_gate_closed.png** (materials): The climax gate reads as a frosted glass shop window: the E-0 leaves are a milky grey veil with a fine screen-door texture, and the swung-open leaf in 26 is a solid pale panel. The same material turns the substation fence on the left of 19 into glass panels, the far E-0 gate in 24 into a white wall, and both coast-road fences in 27 into tan frosted screens (the lower left fence is a flat 41/38/37).
  - Likely cause: WorldMaterialPalette.BuildChainlinkMaterial: transparent blended surface (_SurfaceType 1, cutoff 0.02), metallic 0.82, smoothness 0.45, base colour 0.78/0.80/0.82, plain averaged mips (mipMapsPreserveCoverage false) and fog on transparent. At grazing angles, or beyond a few metres, the wires average into a uniform veil that catches lamp light and haze.
  - Suggested fix: For fences and gate leaves within about 25 m, use alpha-clipped wires (cutoff about 0.5) with coverage-preserving mips and alpha-to-mask under TAA, so the player sees dark wires and a sharp world behind them. Keep the blended veil only for far spans. Galvanised base about 0.5, metallic about 0.5, smoothness about 0.3. E-0 deserves a real gate: tube frame, diagonal brace, mesh or palisade infill, motor housing.
- **[major] 21_ch5_aux_power_floods.png** (lighting): At the aux-power payoff, the one flood in view is a blown-out white card. 72 % of the head's pixels are clipped in all three channels, so the housing, fins and lens do not read. A large grey halo washes the upper frame (sky mean rises from 37 in frame 20 to 61 here), and the trees on the right lose their silhouettes. There is no beam cone, only a glow, and only one flood comes on in frame, so the moment feels small.
  - Likely cause: The lens (MAT_Lens_CoolWhite) runs at LookFixtures.DiffuserNits = 25, about 2x scene white at storm EV 3-4.7. On top of that comes the volumetric scattering right next to a 14000 cd lamp with volumetric 1.80 (LookLights.Zone23, rule ^Light_AuxSubFlood).
  - Suggested fix: Run outdoor flood lenses at about 8-10 nits under the storm exposure, with an LED or reflector grid texture. Set volumetric to about 0.8-1.0 so the light shows as a cone in the rain. Frame or sequence the four AuxSub masts so several floods snap on in view.
- **[major] 27_escape_coast_road.png** (water): The coast road shows no coast. Both sides are tan frosted fences, the end of the road is a dark band with a tiny green sign and a red flare, and the sea is never seen on the escape run. The free-camera coast frame shows it would not help yet: the storm sea is a flat, featureless grey band with no swell, whitecaps or surf catching light, under a flat grey sky broken only by two isolated cloud smudges.
  - Likely cause: Two-metre chain-link fences line both sides (Zone23Dressing / UpgradeChainlinkSpans), with the veil material described above. The storm fog has a 110 m mean free path and a constant colour. WaterWeatherDriver gives the storm sea 48 km/h wind, nightScattering 0.010/0.060/0.065 and grey foam, lit only by a 19 lux moon at shadow dimmer 0.4. The Stormy clouds, at cloudSunDimmer 0.4, show no structure.
  - Suggested fix: Put a low rail on the sea side (like the railing in the coast frame) so the player sees the water while running. Roughen the storm sea (wind 70-90 km/h, foam about 0.9, a lit surf line on the rocks below the road), let lightning light the sea and the foam, and open a gap for the moon somewhere in the cloud deck. Give the road a goal that reads from the gate: a lit assembly-point sign at human scale, or the boat's searchlight sweeping the water.
- **[major] 20_ch5_generator_running.png** (geometry): The lamp post beside the breaker cabinet is mis-assembled. A wall-mount caged bulkhead sits on top of the pole, with its wall plate and bracket hanging in mid-air to the right and the pole running up behind the cage. The glow is a separate orange ring wrapped round the pole, a hand's width below the lamp (also visible in 21). It is the brightest warm object in the main substation frame.
  - Likely cause: In the Substation 3 lamp posts in Zone3_SubstationAndYardBuilder, the lamp_bulkhead model is placed at post + 2.65 m on the pole axis with no rotation or offset. SubPostLamp_Lens_i is a cylinder of radius 0.085 m centred on the 0.055 m pole, 0.105-0.125 m below the lamp, so it shows as a ring around the pole.
  - Suggested fix: Use a pole-top fitting, or turn the bulkhead so its plate sits on the pole face (offset by the plate depth). Make the cage's own glass the glowing part and delete the ring.
- **[major] 23_ch6_yard_entry.png** (materials): The concrete slab that fills the bottom third of the frame looks bone-dry and matte in a downpour (46/39/37): no sheen, no puddles, no lamp streak. The asphalt right beside it mirrors the sodium lamp. The slab reads as a flat, unshaded plane.
  - Likely cause: Probably how WetnessDriver classifies surfaces. It decides wetness per renderer from one probe at the top centre of the bounds (RoofOcclusionMap.IsCovered), so a slab whose centre lies under the workshop roof stays dry. On 'Merged' meshes only IsGroundOnly names count as floor, so a 'concrete' slab gets the wall treatment (wallSheen 0.3). The yard's DockPlatform also uses p.ConcreteBoardform, which is a wall material (Zone3_SubstationAndYardBuilder.BuildLoadingDock).
  - Suggested fix: Decide wet floor per up-facing face, or from several probes across the footprint. Give open slabs ConcreteFloorWet or ConcreteCracked. Check in Play mode with the storm preset that every open slab carries lamp streaks.
- **[minor] 22_ch5_green_route.png** (lighting): The nearest route bollard's ring clips to a mint-white band: 67 % of its pixels hit green 255, mean 186/220/197. Up close, the escape green turns white.
