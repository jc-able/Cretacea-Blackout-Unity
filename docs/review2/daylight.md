# Review 2 (Nov 20, after round 2) — segment `daylight` (score 4/10)

Not yet amazing. At eye level the daylight finally looks natural: the sepia is gone, the skies are lovely and the near plants are convincing. But every distant tree is a dark crossed-card impostor. From the air the 'lush island' looks like a burnt forest, by day the main hill is a black wall, and at dusk it turns into a beige sandstone mountain. The dawn ending is a brown-amber monochrome with muddy clouds and an ochre sea, which is the sepia the owner rejected. Fixing the far-tree impostors and the dawn grade would lift this segment to about a 7.

Strengths:
- Daylight colour at eye level is now natural: blue sky (hue about 212°), neutral white clouds (saturation 0.05 or less), grey-tan paving. The sepia cast of the first build is gone.
- The volumetric cumulus skies are excellent in the coast, valley and yard frames, with believable perspective down to the horizon.
- DuskTransition_valley_eye sells the storm front: a dark cloud deck with sunlit edges, teal shade and the last amber light on the trunks, as the bible asks.
- Near vegetation is convincing: tall grass with dry bases, kapok trees with pale bark and buttress roots, banana and monstera clumps.
- The yard reads as real at eye level: pallets, crates, vehicles, forklift, fuel trailer, lane markings and a readable green SERVICE EXIT sign.
- Nothing clips except the sun and tiny specular specks, and no sky shows banding. The dawn sun disc, its glitter path on the sea and the valley mist glowing into the sun are clean.
- The overlook railing with its long shadows and the rippled sea with a soft horizon look right.

## Findings

- **[blocker] GoldenDay_aerial_sw.png** (vegetation): From the air the jungle reads as a burnt, dead forest. The whole hill mass (left half, right edge, the slopes above the coast) is dark khaki X-shaped sticks with black gaps. Measured far canopy: RGB 0.18/0.18/0.14 (luminance 0.175, saturation 0.24, hue 55°), next to a lawn at luminance 0.395. This frame is front-lit (sun azimuth 248°, camera looking north-east), so this is the impostors' best case. GoldenDay_valley_air (top half) and DawnCoast_aerial_sw show the same thing, and in DuskTransition_aerial_sw the canopy turns orange-brown like an autumn forest. The intro's opening glide and its high crane shot (LookVegetation.CameraLines) will show this.
  - Likely cause: LookVegetationImpostors.cs: the far LOD is two vertical crossed quads with no top view, so seen from 30-60° above each tree collapses into a thin X over the dark ground beneath. The baked 'canopy grade' darkens the albedo before any lighting: leaf texels x0.40/0.84/0.76 in linear, trunk texels x0.24-0.27, then a baked shade of x0.68-1.05. SaveMaterial also makes them LitStandard with smoothness 0 and no transmission. InstancedFoliageRenderer.Submit (line 287) draws every hill plant with lightProbeUsage = LightProbeUsage.Off; check that this is not dropping the sky's ambient light.
  - Suggested fix: Replace the crossed quads with hemi-octahedral impostors (about 8x8 baked views including straight down, with albedo, normal and depth), or at least add a horizontal crown card for views from above. Bake the albedo without the darkening and let lighting and fog do the shading. Keep leaf transmission on the far material and use LightProbeUsage.BlendProbes. Paint the ground under the hill canopy dark green (TL_Moss) instead of brown leaf litter. Target from the air: canopy luminance at least 0.30, saturation at least 0.40, hue 85-110°.
- **[blocker] DuskTransition_meadow_eye.png** (vegetation): The hill filling the top half of the frame reads as a sandstone mountain or a field of rock pillars, not jungle. Under the 5.5° sun the stacked tree cards are beige (RGB 0.435/0.393/0.343, hue 33°, saturation 0.21, no green at all) with blue-grey gaps, in a uniform repeating scale pattern. The same hill in DuskTransition_plaza_eye measures 0.507/0.468/0.422. This breaks Look Bible criterion 3 ('green jungle hills ... never a desert') in the shots that introduce the storm.
  - Likely cause: The same impostors (LookVegetationImpostors.cs). The darkened, desaturated baked albedo cannot hold green under a deep-orange low key light. BuildCrownNormals turns every card into a dome facing the sun (shape 0.72 + leaf 0.28, tilted 0.30 toward the sky). The 4.5 m canopy grid of near-identical cards (LookVegetation.ScatterTerrainTrees) creates the regular pattern.
  - Suggested fix: The same impostor rebuild, plus per-instance colour variation (about ±8 % brightness and ±5° hue, through an instanced colour property) and 2-3 impostor variants per species. Keep the trunk band hidden under the crowns. Check on this frame: the hill's hue should stay at 70° or more (olive-gold), never tan.
- **[blocker] DawnCoast_coast_eye.png** (colour): The ending view is a brown-amber monochrome, the 'sepia' the owner rejected. Frame mean RGB is 0.295/0.212/0.117. The upper sky is muddy brown (0.267/0.214/0.176) instead of pale blue. The backlit clouds are khaki-brown with no cool shadow side. The sea is orange-brown (0.314/0.205/0.095) instead of teal. The lower left of the frame (grass and pavement) is crushed to pure black: 36 % of the two bottom-left grid cells (160x180 px each) are at RGB 6 or below. The Look Bible target is a peach to pale-blue sky and teal water.
  - Likely cause: AtmosphereController.GetKeyLook sets DawnCoast elevationDeg to 4.5, so the physically based sky turns the whole key light deep orange. In LookAtmosphereProfiles, dawn evCompensation −0.6 protects the sun and pushes the rest of the sky down into dim brown. Temperature +1, tint +3, smhHighlights (1.06, 1.0, 0.95) and saturation +10 add more warmth, with nothing cool to balance it. zenithTint (0.82, 0.90, 1.0) is too weak to keep blue at the top of a frame aimed at the horizon.
  - Suggested fix: Put the warmth in the light, not the grade. Dawn temperature −5 to −8; cool split-tone shadows (splitShadows about (0.42, 0.50, 0.62)); zenithTint about (0.62, 0.80, 1.0). Raise the sun to 6-8° (or lower the dawn aerosol density) so the key light is peach-gold rather than orange. Use evCompensation about −0.2, with the exposure histogram's upper percentile set to ignore the sun disc. Let the sea outside the glitter path show its teal scattering. Target: top of frame hue 200° or more, peach-pink cloud undersides, nothing in the foreground below RGB 8.
- **[major] GoldenDay_meadow_eye.png** (vegetation): In full afternoon sun, the jungle hill behind the Visitor Center is a near-black olive mass: luminance 0.148, saturation 0.11 here, and luminance 0.18 in GoldenDay_plaza_eye. That is darker than the shaded leaves 10 m from the camera, so distance runs backwards (no aerial perspective) and the island's signature hill becomes a black wall instead of lush green. The sun (azimuth 248°) is behind and to the left of these north-facing cameras, so this face should be lit. The jungle wall behind the Visitor Center's west wing in the plaza view is just as black-green.
  - Likely cause: The hill is the impostor jungle (see the aerial finding). The GoldenDay haze is also very thin (fogMeanFreePath 5200 m), so nothing lifts the distance toward the sky colour.
  - Suggested fix: Rebuild the impostors first. Then add a little more daytime haze (mean free path about 2500-3000 m, pale-blue albedo) so the hill at 300-600 m sits at luminance 0.30-0.40 with a blue-green cast, lighter than the foreground shade.
- **[major] DawnCoast_valley_eye.png** (colour): At dawn the whole island goes amber-olive monochrome. In DawnCoast_aerial_sw the forest is a saturated brown (0.209/0.133/0.025), the meadow and the lake become the same olive (lake 0.180/0.136/0.039), and the sea is grey-ochre.   - Suggested fix: Apply the same dawn retune. Keep the shade cool blue-green (smhShadows about (0.90, 0.98, 1.12) plus the split tone), so the grass stays green in shadow and only sunlit edges and the mist turn gold.
- **[major] GoldenDay_valley_eye.png** (colour): The day's greens are yellow-olive, not tropical. Tall grass is RGB 0.297/0.299/0.065 (hue 60°), meadow grass is 0.296/0.297/0.110 (hue 60°, GoldenDay_meadow_eye), palm fronds are hue 55° and banana leaves hue 56° (plaza and meadow). Red equals green on every plant, so the 'lush' island reads khaki or mustard, and the orange flecks in the meadow ground read as a dry late summer. The Look Bible asks for 'saturated tropical greens'.
  - Likely cause: The plant and grass albedos are yellow-green: LookVegetationImpostors notes that the palm atlases have R about equal to G, and the TL_Grass tint is white on tj_grass_BaseColor. They are lit by a warm 27° sun through the physically based sky, and GoldenDay saturation +10 amplifies the yellow.
  - Suggested fix: Shift vegetation toward green at the material level: plant _BaseColor in LookMaterials about (0.80, 1.0, 0.82), TL_Grass and TL_Moss tint about (0.85, 1.0, 0.85). Alternatively, add a GoldenDay Hue-vs-Hue curve that moves hues 50-65° to 75-90°. Cut the orange flecks in tj_grass_BaseColor. Target: sunlit grass with G/R of 1.25 or more, hue 80-100°.
- **[major] GoldenDay_valley_air.png** (vegetation): An obvious LOD seam. Trees within about 60 m of the camera are lit and green with pale trunks (luminance 0.30, saturation 0.49); every tree beyond is a dark grey-brown stick (luminance 0.18, saturation 0.12), giving a band where the forest seems to die. Between them the lawn is a flat lime fill (hue 75°, saturation 0.54) with no large-scale variation, so the valley looks like a golf course next to a burnt forest.
  - Likely cause: The impostor is far darker than the mesh LODs (canopy grade, no transmission). For the instanced hill trees, LookVegetationPrefabs.InstancedLods(stem, 0.35f, 1f) ends the mesh LODs at 35 % of their authored distance, so the impostor takes over close to the camera. The terrain uses TL_Grass at a 7.5 m tile with no large-scale tint.
  - Suggested fix: Match the impostor's luminance and saturation to LOD1 within ±10 % at the switch distance under GoldenDay. Give the hill trees a longer mesh LOD range near the play area again. Add a low-frequency tint and noise layer to the meadow terrain paint: darker damp patches, worn trails, flowering patches.
- **[major] GoldenDay_valley_eye.png** (weather): A flat brown-grey slab (RGB 0.32/0.31/0.25), about 2 m tall with a hard top edge, lies across the right half of the frame at the foot of the kapok trees (y about 393-412, x about 760-1280) and hides their buttress roots. - **[major] GoldenDay_yard_eye.png** (materials): The stacked shipping containers (right third of the frame) read as boxes clad in planks, not corrugated steel: flat 10 cm boards with dark gaps and no shading from a trapezoid profile. - **[minor] GoldenDay_coast_eye.png** (exposure): Sunlit whites stop short of white. The cumulus average 0.57 (95th percentile 0.70), and in all seven day frames the brightest 1 % of pixels reaches only 0.66-0.79. The afternoon looks slightly grey and milky rather than bright tropical sun.
  - Likely cause: The shared Custom tone-curve shoulder (shoulderStrength 0.9, shoulderLength 2.4, shoulderAngle 0.2 in LookAtmosphereProfiles baseLook) was built so night hot spots keep their texture, but it also compresses the day.
  - Suggested fix: Give GoldenDay its own shoulder (length about 1.2-1.5, strength about 0.7). The change would blend during the sunset while the key light is below the horizon. Alternatively add +0.3 EV of exposure compensation. Target cloud tops at 0.85-0.92.
- **[minor] DuskTransition_plaza_eye.png** (lighting): The lamp head at the top centre shows its lens as a flat, uniform salmon-pink rectangle with no diffuser gradient, no hot core and no glow. It reads as a painted sticker, and the colour is pink rather than sodium or tungsten amber.
  - Likely cause: The lens plane has a single flat emissive colour (LookFixtures lens, 25 nits).
  - Suggested fix: Use an emissive texture with a bright core and a darker rim, colour it 2200-2700 K amber, and give it enough nits at dusk exposure for a faint bloom.
