# Review 2 second opinion — segment `night_a`

First critic 6/10, second critic 5.5/10. Kept 12, dropped 3, added 3.

## Kept (confirmed in the image)

- **[major] NightStormBlackout_valley_eye.png** (exposure / tonal range): Every open-ground storm frame sits in one narrow dark band. Luma percentiles I measured: the storm valley runs from 9.7 (1st) to 75 (99.9th), the storm meadow's 99th is 53, the 99th of 18_ch4_trex_reveal is 69 and the aerial's 99.9th is 63. In the storm valley the road (19-28/255), the grass (20-28), the right-hand trunks (26) and the upper sky (29) all fall within about 10 levels of each other, and the low sky is 43. Only the small lit building at the end of the road rises above that. One correction: the moon does have a direction, because the right-hand tall grass is rim-lit from the moon side (28 against 20 on the left). It is still far too weak to model the trees or the ground, so the valley and the meadow read as a grey veil and fail criterion 1.
  - Seen: I measured the percentiles and region means myself. At normal view the storm valley, the storm meadow and 18 are murky blue-grey with no specular highlight anywhere. NightCalm_valley_eye reads better only because of its moonlit cloud rims (99.9th = 135).
  - Fix: LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs, storm look (it copies night): raise contrast from 6 to 12-16, toeStrength from 0 to 0.05, and lower indirectDiffuse (inherited 2.0) to about 1.3. AtmosphereController.cs GetKeyLook(NightStormBlackout): raise shadowDimmer from 0.4 to 0.6. Trim the constant haze glow (storm.fogColor x3.0 at a 110 m mean free path) by about 30% so the midground stops lifting to the same grey as the sky. Give every open view one highlight: a wet glint, a distant practical or a strike. Targets: darks under the canopy at 12/255 or below, something in the frame at 120/255 or above.
- **[major] 18_ch4_trex_reveal.png** (wet ground / materials): In a downpour the valley's gravel road is matte and looks dry: a flat 20-22/255 in 17 and 18, the same in both valley presets. There are no puddles, no ruts and no glints in the torch shaft, even in NightCalm_valley_eye, where the moon (azimuth 115, elevation 31) sits ahead-right of the view specifically to make that glint. Two frames earlier the service-path concrete is mirror-wet, so the valley reads like a different weather. Cause: TerrainWetnessDriver.ProfileFor("Gravel") caps smoothness at 0.42 (add 0.10) to avoid the review-1 hoar-frost sparkle, and every puddle decal (PathPuddle_1-4 and DressServicePathsLane in Zone2_ServicePathsBuilder.cs) stops at the Paddock 4 gate (x about 92).
  - Seen: In 17 and 18 heavy rain streaks fall onto a road that reads as dry grey gravel (region means 20.5 and 22.0). In the code there is no water on the valley road.
  - Fix: Keep the pebble field at 0.42 and add standing water where water collects. Put DecalPuddleWet/WxPuddle decals in the ruts and low points along the Service Road 3 polyline, 2-5 m long, every 15-25 m. Add two dark glossy rut strips (smoothness 0.75-0.85) and mud at the verges, so the moon and the torch have something to catch.
- **[major] 18_ch4_trex_reveal.png** (creature staging (T. rex only)): The T. rex reveal shows no T. rex. On a gamma-lifted, 2-5x zoomed copy of the horizon I found road, tall grass, trees, the lit building (x 830, y 315) and a small bush (x 555, y 318), and no animal. By the code it cannot read here. It spawns at trex_drink (192,0,40) and patrols away towards (212,0,40), so at the capture pose (128,0) it is 75-90 m off: about 35 px tall at 720p and dark against a dark treeline. TrexStreamRevealRoutine shows it only during three short backlit strikes (t = 0, 0.45 and 2.05 s), and the team's capture missed all three, so the game's signature beat is unproven.
  - Seen: The frame, plus the code: WorldRegistry trex_drink, StoryDirector TrexPatrolPointsXZ and TrexStreamRevealRoutine.
  - Fix: Stage the reveal 30-45 m from the trigger, or route the animal across the lit stream mist or in front of the lit building at (830, 315), so it reads as a silhouette between strikes too. Add a dim moon-side rim on the animal. Capture the beat on a strike frame to prove it reads.
- **[major] 17_ch4_valley_entry.png** (vegetation): The valley has one canopy-tree model, repeated at the same proportions, upright and evenly spaced over open lawn: three side by side at x 720-830 in 17, five at x 100, 530, 700, 1050 and 1230 in 18, five identical silhouettes in the lifted storm-valley sky, and the same tree dotted evenly over the lawn in the aerial. It reads as a savanna of baobabs, not jungle. Cause: LookVegetation.cs scales trees uniformly (localScale = Vector3.one * scale; jungle_tree 0.62-1.05) and never leans them.
  - Seen: The repetition is obvious at a glance in 17, in 18 and in both valley presets.
  - Fix: Scale the crown non-uniformly (0.75-1.35, independent of height), lean each tree 4-8 degrees and cluster them into overlapping groves with root clusters, vines and an understorey (banana, tree fern, monstera), so the valley edge becomes a continuous jungle wall.
- **[major] 15_ch3_path_raptor_crossing.png** (architecture dressing): Frame 15 is a canyon of three plain wall faces (about 70-80% of the frame) with one vine and a lamp; frame 14 is the same apart from a wall lamp and a kerb. The walls are textured panels, not literal boxes. Correction: 16 is not like this (see dropped).
  - Seen: The walls in 15 are smooth beige-grey planes. The torch hot spot on the back wall shows almost no texture.
  - Fix: Do what the first critic proposed, but with bigger, higher-contrast forms that read in silhouette and under the warm bulkheads: conduit runs with junction boxes, wall signs (PADDOCK 4 with an arrow, bay stencils), razor wire on the coping, jungle hanging over the wall, and a door or a pilaster light per run. Re-shoot the plaza, meadow, valley and aerial before anyone judges the rain or the blackout from them.

## Added by the second critic

- **[minor] 14_ch3_service_path_start.png** (viewmodel / shadows): The sleeve of the held arm shows a regular stair-step ladder of light lines (x 880-1000, y 590-680), the staircase of a low-resolution local-light shadow sampled on the arm. It is fainter in 15 and 16, absent where no shadow-casting lamp is near (13, 17, 18). The arm is half a metre from the lens, so one shadow texel covers many pixels, and the steps will crawl as the arm sways; the arm is on screen the whole game. The likely source is the wall bulkhead (LookLights.Zone23.cs rule ^Light_Paths\w*Bulkhead_Batt$, Shadow.Low), sampled on the arm drawn by ViewmodelCustomPass.
  - Fix: Stop the arm sampling local-light shadow maps: put the viewmodel on its own light layer lit by shadowless copies or a fill that takes the nearest lamp's colour. Or give the nearest lamps a higher shadow resolution and normal bias inside about 6 m (LampShadowBudget).
- **[minor] 13_ch3_loading_dock.png** (wetness coherence): The floor under the dock canopy is soaked: the torch hot spot mirrors and sparkles on it (x 700-1100, y 560-720) as on the open service lane, although it is under a RainBlocker roof that WetnessDriver deliberately keeps dry.   - Fix: Use a dry asphalt or ConcreteFloor under the roof, with a wet-strip decal only along the open east and south edges where rain blows in.

## Dropped

- Raptor half of finding 3: '15 shows an empty junction; players would get the beat as audio and text only'.
  - Why not: The code stages the raptor running through that junction 9 m in front of the capture pose: TriggerServicePathRaptorCrossing goes from (64,0,30) to (64,0,-6) with ScriptMoveTo at a speed of 7 m/s, so the animal is in view for about 1 s, along the raptor-print decals. route_test.py shoots 2.5 s after the trigger plus command latency, after the animal has passed. The empty frame is a capture-timing artefact, not evidence that the beat lacks a creature. A timed capture is still worth doing: the end point (64,0,-6) lies beyond the Seg4_SW wall, so check the animal does not run into the concrete.
- Finding 5's claim that 14 and 16 look like 15.
  - Why not: 16 is the best-dressed frame in the chapter: legible PADDOCK 4 SERVICE GATE and DANGER HIGH VOLTAGE plates, hazard-striped jambs and kerb, perforated gate leaves, a fallen trunk and visible grime on the right wall. Only 14 and 15 are bare.
- NightCalm half of finding 12: the valley cumulus 'reads as a daytime cloud'.
  - Why not: The bright rims (90-135/255) are where the moon (azimuth 115, elevation 31, just above the frame's top-right) lights the cloud edges. The navy sky and the stars keep it night, and this is the best-ranged outdoor frame of the segment (1st to 99.9th percentile: 10-135). It is one isolated blob, but it does not read as day.
