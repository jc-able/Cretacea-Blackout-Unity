# Review 2 (Nov 20, after round 2) — segment `night_a` (score 6/10)

Clearly better than round 1. The outdoor nights now have natural colour and can be read and played: nothing is crushed to black, there is no teal or red wash, warm practicals make pools, the concrete looks wet, rain reads as rain in Play mode, and the flashlight shows texture. The NightCalm plaza and the service gate come close to the Jurassic Park mood. It is not amazing yet. The open-ground storm in Tyrant Valley and the meadow is a flat grey veil, the terrain looks dry, and one cloned tree species fills the valley. The service path is bare slab walls, and neither creature beat shows a creature. The free-camera storm captures are corrupted by the player-centred rain box and by lamps left on, so nobody has reviewed the real blackout plaza.

Strengths:
- No frame is crushed: the 1st percentile is 6-10/255 in all 13 frames and almost no pixels fall below 3/255. Trees, palms and the hill stand out against the sky in every flashlight-off outdoor frame (both meadows, both valleys, 17, 18).
- The night palette is natural and coherent: blue-grey sky, neutral ground, warm 2400-3600 K practicals, no teal or blue cast. Red appears only as small beacons (16) and amber as hazard light (the jeep in 17).
- NightCalm_plaza_eye is the hero frame: a real cobra-head fixture throws a warm sodium pool on the paving, the lit shopfronts, signs, palms and hill silhouette work together, and puddles in the mid-ground mirror the sign.
- Wet concrete in Play mode convinces: the lane mirrors the lamp as a warm streak (14), and the flashlight pools glint on wet asphalt (13, 15, 16). No rain falls under the dock canopy.
- Rain reads as rain in the HUD frames: thin, wind-slanted streaks show against the sky and in the beam (14-18), with no snow look.
- The flashlight works as intended: a soft volumetric shaft and a hot spot that shows the board-form concrete (15) and the perforated gate mesh (16), not a white disc.
- The storytelling props and signage are good: a fallen trunk through the gate leaf, the overturned jeep with its interior glow and jerry cans, legible PADDOCK 4 / DANGER HIGH VOLTAGE signs with hazard paint, and buttress-root trees at the dock and the valley entry.
- The objective panel and the caption boxes share one clean style across all route frames.

## Findings

- **[major] NightStormBlackout_valley_eye.png** (exposure): In the open-ground storm frames the whole picture sits in a narrow dark band. Measured: the storm valley's 99.9th percentile is 75/255, the storm meadow's 99th is 53, 18_ch4_trex_reveal's 99th is 69 (HUD text excluded) and the aerial's 99.9th is 63. Grass, gravel road and tree trunks all sit at the same ~20-25/255. There is no direction to the moonlight, no glint and no highlight anywhere, so Tyrant Valley and the meadow read as a dark grey veil rather than a storm, which fails acceptance criterion 1 (full tonal range). NightCalm_valley_eye is better only because the moonlit cloud and grass tips give it something bright.
  - Likely cause: The storm is lit almost only by ambient light under a uniform haze. The storm look inherits night.indirectDiffuse = 2.0 (LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs line 259), which doubles the sky fill. Against that, the moon is weak: lux 19 and shadowDimmer 0.4 (AtmosphereController.GetKeyLook). On top of this, storm.contrast is 6 (line 283), fogMeanFreePath 110 m (line 279) and fogColor is multiplied by 3.0 (line 277).
  - Suggested fix: For the storm outdoors, lower indirectDiffuse to about 1.3, raise contrast to 12-16 with toeStrength 0.05 (within the bible's 0-0.10), and raise the moon's shadowDimmer to about 0.6 so the ground under the canopy drops below the haze and the trees model. Give every open-ground view one highlight: wet speculars (see the next finding), one distant practical (a watchtower strobe or a battery lamp at the stream), and lightning. Targets: the darks under the canopy at 12/255 or below, and something in the frame at 120/255 or above.
- **[major] 18_ch4_trex_reveal.png** (materials): The moon never lights wet ground in this segment. In a downpour the gravel road and the lawns look dry and matte. Measured: the road is a flat ~23/255 in 17, 18 and both valley presets. There are no puddles in the ruts and no glints in the flashlight hot spot, even in NightCalm_valley_eye, where the moon was placed ahead-right of the view specifically to make that glint. Next to the soaked concrete of the service path, the terrain looks inconsistent.
  - Likely cause: TerrainWetnessDriver.ProfileFor caps gravel at smoothness add 0.10 / max 0.42 / albedo 0.80 and grass at 0.10 / 0.40, after review 1's frost-sparkle complaint. LookWeather.IsPuddleSurface (line 337) accepts only hits whose collider has a Renderer with a named material. Service Road 3 is the TL_Gravel terrain layer (LookTerrainLayers RoadGravel), so it never gets puddle decals.
  - Suggested fix: Place WxPuddle decals on terrain hits where the gravel or mud splat weight is above 0.6 along the Service Road 3 polyline (ruts and low points). Add two dark, glossy rut strips (smoothness 0.75-0.85) along the road and keep the pebble field at 0.42. Let backlit grass catch a specular moon rim, without lifting the albedo.
- **[major] 18_ch4_trex_reveal.png** (creatures): Neither creature beat shows a creature. The T. rex reveal frame shows no T. rex anywhere: I checked a gamma-lifted copy along the whole horizon and found only road, grass, trees, rain and the subtitle 'Something massive is moving by the stream.' 15_ch3_path_raptor_crossing.png shows an empty junction. Players would get the beat as audio and text only.
  - Likely cause: StoryDirector.TrexStreamRevealRoutine relies on three lightning flashes (at 0, 0.45 and 2.05 s) to silhouette an animal 75-110 m away. Between flashes it sits at about 20/255 inside haze with a 110 m mean free path. route_test.py takes the shot at 2.2 s, between flashes. The raptor runs raptor_cross_a to raptor_cross_b, probably behind the 5 m walls.
  - Suggested fix: Give the reveal a read that lasts: route the T. rex in front of the lit stream mist or the distant lit building (x≈830, y≈315), or add a dim rim light that follows it. Then capture the beat on a flash frame to prove it reads. Stage the raptor across the lane or along the wall coping under the corner fault lamp, inside the view.
- **[major] 17_ch4_valley_entry.png** (vegetation): The valley has one canopy tree, cloned. Every valley frame shows 5-8 identical umbrella silhouettes at the same proportions, upright and evenly spaced over open lawn. In 17 three clones stand side by side at x≈720-830; in 18 they repeat at x≈100, 530, 700, 1050 and 1230. Tyrant Valley and the meadow read as a savanna of copies, not jungle.
  - Likely cause: LookVegetationPrefabs.Specs (line 40) has a single broadleaf tree, jungle_tree, and Assets/Art has no other. LookVegetation.cs line 209 places it with uniform scale (Vector3.one * scale).
  - Suggested fix: Scale each jungle_tree non-uniformly (crown width 0.75-1.35, independent of height) and lean it 4-8 degrees. Cluster the trees into overlapping groves with root_cluster_01 at the base, hanging_vines in the crowns and a banana_plant / tree_fern / monstera understorey, so the valley edge becomes a continuous jungle wall instead of a row of lollipop trees.
- **[major] 15_ch3_path_raptor_crossing.png** (geometry): The service path is a canyon of bare 5 m precast slabs. In 15 three blank walls fill about 70% of the frame, with one vine as the only detail; 14 and 16 are the same. It reads as a blockout maze, not a park service route, and breaks the 'no bare boxes' rule in the look bible.
  - Likely cause: Zone2_ServicePathsBuilder.cs lines 73-88: the PanelWall runs (WallH 5 m, WallStyle.Path) have panels, coping and a kerb, but almost nothing is dressed on the lane faces in these views.
  - Suggested fix: Dress every run with rain-runoff streaks and algae decals under the coping, moss along the kerb, conduit with junction boxes, two or three wall signs (PADDOCK 4 →, AUTHORISED VEHICLES, bay stencils), razor wire or fence on the coping, and foliage hanging over from the jungle side. Break up the long runs with a door or a pilaster light.
- **[major] NightStormBlackout_plaza_eye.png** (weather): The free-camera storm frames are broken and misleading, and the owner judges from them. (1) Hard vertical seams in the sky where the rain stops: in the storm plaza at x≈735 from the frame top to the roofline, the left side has streaks and lighter haze while the right side is 8-10 levels darker with no rain; in the storm meadow at x≈706 and x≈948; in the NightCalm meadow at x≈945. (2) No rain at all in the storm valley. (3) A glowing translucent cylinder over the visitor centre in NightStormBlackout_aerial_sw. (4) The 'blackout' plaza has every lamp and the visitor centre lit, and averages exactly the same as the calm plaza (mean RGB 45/40/38 in both). So the real blackout plaza has never been reviewed.
  - Likely cause: StormRainOcclusionController keeps the 34x17x34 m streak box (StreakBoxSize, lines 57-60) and the 55/110/180 m RainCurtains (line 66, positioned on Camera.main at line 210) on the player, while `cretacea capture` renders from a separate __CretaceaCliCaptureCamera (CretaceaCliTools.cs line 84). Tools/lookdev/presetshots.sh only calls SetPreset, so the light groups stay powered on (Plaza => !blackout in ApplyEditModeLightGroupState is never applied). It also waits 4 s, while WetnessDriver needs 15 s to soak.
  - Suggested fix: In presetshots.sh, move the player or main camera to each capture pose, or call SetFollowTarget(captureCam) and re-centre the curtains. Apply the blackout light state together with the storm preset and wait at least 15 s before shooting. Then re-shoot the storm plaza, meadow and valley. Until then, do not judge rain or the blackout from these frames.

- **[minor] 17_ch4_valley_entry.png** (geometry): The overturned jeep seems to hover about 25 cm above the gravel at its near edge.
