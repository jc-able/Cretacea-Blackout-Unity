# Review 2 (Nov 20, after round 2) — segment `intro` (score 5/10)

Not amazing yet. The colour and exposure work landed: the day frames look natural with no clipping or crushed blacks, there are no black or white frames at the cuts, and the gallery and office interiors are believable. The cinematic still fails at its own job, though. The dinosaurs it is selling are hidden (the sauropod) or framed badly (the T. rex roars at the frame edge behind a mullion), only 2-4 visitors make up the "crowd", the aerial shows blockout boxes, and the night reveal of the plaza is a clipped white rectangle full of broken reflections. Fixing the probe/SSR white-out and re-blocking shots 3, 4 and 6 would get this from about 5 to 7+.

Strengths:
- Day colour is natural now (intro_01-07): blue sky, white cumulus, saturated greens. Sunlit paving is warm (R/B about 1.33) and shade is blue (about 0.82). No pixel is clipped, nothing is crushed, and the old sepia cast is gone.
- Exposure across day, dusk and night is controlled: mean luminance steps down smoothly (about 0.12-0.16 linear by day, 0.05 at dusk, 0.02-0.05 at night). No cut lands on a black or blown frame, so the sunset/moonrise dip logic works.
- intro_15 is a pretty beat: the last amber light on the jungle ridge above a teal-shaded plaza with blue haze between the trees.
- Viewing gallery (intro_10-13): the board-formed concrete interior is balanced against the bright jungle with no blown window. The T. rex model reads well, with a warm sunlit hide and an open-mouthed roar.
- Visitor Center facade dressing reads as a real park: the WELCOME TO CRETACEA PARK sign, logo roundel, MEET REX banner, queue stanchions, park map board and bollards.
- Security office (intro_18-20) is believable: troffer grid ceiling, CCTV wall with live feeds, cork board, island map, filing cabinet, and a warm desk pendant against cool tubes, with a full tonal range. The title shot hands off to the first gameplay frame from the same camera, with no jump.
- Title typography is consistent (white condensed heading over an amber mono line), the letterbox and skip prompt are clean, and no sky or wall gradient shows banding.

## Findings

- **[blocker] intro_16.png** (exposure): The night aerial, the moment the park lights come on (between the listed 15 and 17), is broken. The wet paving in front of the Visitor Center is a clipped white rectangle (mean 222/255, max 255, 0.7% of the frame clipped) with hard left and right edges, full of ragged, blocky reflection fragments of the palms, kiosks and facade. It is about 3x brighter than the lobby it would be mirroring (facade interior 77/66/57), so it reflects nothing in the night scene. intro_15 already shows the same rectangle as a cold blue-white wash (R/B 0.84) before any plaza lamp is on.
  - Likely cause: Most likely the 'Plaza' outdoor reflection probe. Its box (LookProbes.cs line 23: centre (-32,-46), 74x8x22 m) matches the rectangle exactly, and it still holds the end-of-dusk capture while the night exposure (EV 3.3-5) is already in place. AtmosphereController.BlendRoutine re-renders outdoor probes only every 2.5 s and at the end of a blend, and the 4.5 s night glide cuts away before the night capture. Soaked slabs go to smoothness 0.85-0.94 (WetnessDriver slabSmoothnessMinCap/Max) and SSR admits anything above 0.70, so screen-space hits draw dark palms and every miss falls back to the stale bright probe. The comment near AtmosphereController line 491 describes this exact 'bright rectangle on the wet plaza'.
  - Suggested fix: In BlendRoutine, call BumpAtmosphereEpoch() / ReflectionProbeRefresh.RequestAllOutdoor() when the dip swaps sun for moon (k crosses 0.5) and again when lookK reaches 1. Let the plaza probe skip the one-probe-per-frame queue during cutscenes. Until the night capture lands, fade the outdoor probes' multiplier toward 0 so misses fall back to the dark sky. Raise the plaza probe's horizontal blend distance from 4 m to about 10 m, and cap wet paving at about 0.80 so it reads as a sheen, not a mirror. Then check that the 1300 cd Light_PlazaLamp pools read as warm pools from 30 m up; today they are pinpricks.
- **[major] intro_13.png** (creatures): The intro's money shot misses. Through the whole 6 s gallery shot (intro_11, intro_12) the window shows only trees, with the T. rex a sliver at the left edge outside the wall. When it roars (intro_13) it sits in the left third, body cropped by the frame edge, a black mullion through its chest, head in profile walking past the glass instead of confronting the crowd. The camera is centred on empty jungle. (The frame captions are out of date: 07 is the facade, 09 the sauropod rail, 13 the roar, 14 the 'Closing time' aerial.)
  - Likely cause: StoryDirector.OpeningCutsceneRoutine spawns the T. rex at (64,-28) and calls ScriptMoveTo((66,-52), 2.3). At 2.3 m/s that is about 10.5 s of walking, longer than shots 4 and 4b together (6.0 + 3.8 s). Both cameras aim at the end mark (63-66, 4-5.5, -40..-54), and the roar fires at 6.0 s, when the animal is still about 10 m short of it.
  - Suggested fix: Start the T. rex at about (65,-38), or raise its speed to about 4 m/s, so it reaches the middle pane by about 4.5 s. Stop it facing the glass and the visitors, then roar. Aim shot 4b at the head (or lerp the look target with the animal) and frame the head between mullions. Add faint glass reflections or smudges so the crowd is visibly behind glass.

- **[major] intro_17.png** (geometry): The first night exterior is a flat-on elevation of a featureless dark box. About 60% of the frame is a wall at 17-24/255 under a plain slab roof, with one centred window and a small wall pack: no door, pipe, sign or trim, no rain, and no reflection or drops on the glass.   - Likely cause: StoryDirector shot 6 pushes straight in along the wall's normal, from (-86,3.2,27) to (-73.8,1.85,28.4). The Security Office exterior is an undressed box, and its only lamp is Light_SecWest_WallPack (600 cd).

Dress the wall: downpipe, AC unit, conduit, a door with a lit SECURITY plate, cables. Put rain streaks in the wall pack's cone and drops and runs on the window, and let the window light spill onto the wet ground. Add a cool moonlit rim so the roofline separates from the sky.

- **[major] intro_14.png** (geometry): The 'Closing time' aerial, the only overview of the park (intro_14-16), reads as a blockout. The Visitor Center wings are flat boxes with blank roofs. The plaza is one uniform tile grid with sparse palms in planters that become pure black discs at dusk and night (intro_15, intro_16). The lamp posts are tiny, and there is no roof equipment, no signage seen from above and nobody in it.
  - Likely cause: Plaza and roofs are built from flat primitives (Zone1_VisitorCenterBuilder plaza section around line 2446, LookRoofs.cs), and the planter soil albedo is near black.

Lift the planter soil to about 0.12-0.15 albedo with ground cover. Or reframe the shot lower and tighter so the facade carries it instead of the roofs.
- **[minor] intro_17.png** (weather): No rain falls in any night shot of the intro: no streaks in the wall pack's light (intro_17) and none over the plaza (intro_16). Meanwhile the radio says the storm is rolling in and the caption reads [THUNDER CRACKS OVERHEAD].

- **[minor] intro_11.png** (materials): Pale, offset halos surround the visitors' legs on the glossy gallery floor.
  - Likely cause: An SSR occlusion artefact: where the legs block rays toward the bright window, the probe fallback shows through (SSR minSmoothness 0.70).
  - Suggested fix: Lower the gallery floor's smoothness below 0.70 so it uses probe reflections only, or raise the SSR object thickness.
- **[minor] intro_20.png** (lighting): Stair-stepped shadow bands climb the right wall of the security office in the first gameplay frame (also in intro_19).
  - Likely cause: The troffers are 176-degree spots (LookLights.Zone1.cs TrofferCone) with cached shadow maps of 512 (Medium) or 256 (Low) (LookLights.cs around line 202). At grazing angles on the side walls each shadow texel is huge.
  - Suggested fix: Turn shadows off on the Security office's _Grid companion troffers. Give Light_Security_Main 1024 with soft filtering, or a narrower shadow-casting cone, and rely on contact shadows for the props.
- **[minor] intro_18.png** (geometry): During the window push-in, two translucent grey rectangles float in front of the cork board and the wall to its right.
