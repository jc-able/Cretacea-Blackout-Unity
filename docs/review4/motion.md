# Review 4 (Nov 20, final state) — segment `motion` (first critic 6/10, second critic 5.5/10)

Animation in motion: the first-person hand, the dinosaurs, the visitors

## Verdict

Not AAA yet. The new gloved hand and its spring motion are a real step up from the mitten: the bob is smooth with no pops, and the T. rex model with planted feet reads well. But the glove is lit from above by a light that isn't there, the torch looks as if it is held backwards, the T. rex snaps into a dated upright roar, turns 90° in about 0.4 s and walks with its jaw always open, and the raptor never appears in its clip, so its run is still unjudged. Review 3 had no motion segment. Of the related review 3 majors from other segments, the mullion through the roaring head (intro_13) and the T. rex missing from the valley (night_a 18) are fixed. The empty raptor junction (night_a 15) is still there. The merged trikes (intro_09) can't be judged here, because only one trike's crest shows. Sprint, crouch, the switch press and the trike walk are not in this evidence.

## Strengths

- The held hand is now a real gloved hand round a whole tactical torch, with a sleeve running to the frame edge. No mitten, and nothing ends in mid-air. In the lit office it reads as worn leather with a sheen (knuckles mean 51/255).
- The viewmodel motion is smooth and alive. The 30 fps walk (hand_walk) tracks as a figure-eight bob of about ±7 px sideways and 5 px vertical, with no jitter, pops or snapping. The idle breathes.
- The T. rex model reads superbly in sun and under lightning: skin detail, wet back, arms and teeth (trex_walk_side f_11). Its feet stay on the same pixels through the head-down hold and the whole roar (intro_trex f_12-f_20): no sliding, no floating, no sinking.
- Review 3's intro major is fixed: the roaring head stays 60-120 px clear of the right mullion from f_17 to f_24, and the face is sharp.
- Review 3's valley major is fixed: in all 32 trex_reveal frames the T. rex stands as a clean silhouette with a moon rim against the lit haze, and it crosses the gap at a speed that matches its stride (its edge moves about 5-6 px per frame).
- The visitors walk naturally, with arm swing, real stride length and contact shadows (the woman in lilac, the child in purple, visitors f_05-f_24). No T-poses, no clipping, no skating visible.

## Confirmed findings (12) and missed by the first critic (2)

- **[major] hand_walk_sheet.jpg (also trex_reveal f_10, raptor_cross f_16)** (first-person hand: lighting): In every night frame the back of the glove is lit from above by a light that has no source in the scene. It reads as crinkled grey foil or a plastic bag, not black leather, and it is the brightest near object in the lower right. The fingers under the barrel stay black, so the light plainly comes from above. In the lit office it is fine.
  - Fix: Cause: ViewmodelFill is a child of the beam with a fixed local offset (-0.10, 0.22, -0.42) (FlashlightController.cs line 273). After the round 5 model swap that puts it about 0.31 m above and behind the new glove's knuckles. 18 cd there is about 190 lux with the beam on (line 472), and the beam-off 0.3x is 5.4 cd, about 56 lux, against 10-14 lux of moonlight. The 18 cd was calibrated on the old mitten ('20/255'). The critic's fix is right: put the fill in front of and below the hand, 0.5-0.7 m away, at 2-4 cd, so the glove holds 15-30/255 with knuckle sheen at 80 or less. With the beam off outdoors, fade it to 0. Tint it to the ambient. Keep about 1 lux indoors in the blackout so the glove does not fall back to review 3's 6/255. Re-measure on the new rig.
- **[major] hand_idle_sheet.jpg (f_01; same in hand_walk, raptor_cross, trex_reveal)** (first-person hand: torch model and pose): The torch is held nearly end-on. Its tail faces the eye as a hollow black hole about 85 px across, ringed by silver crenellation teeth, so the nearest and largest shape of the viewmodel reads as a muzzle or a pipe pointing at the player. The barrel reads as a black tube rather than a flashlight.
  - Fix: Use the critic's fix: grip 6-8 cm right and 4-6 cm lower with the aim kept on the crosshair, so the barrel shows its side. Let the palm or thumb cover the tail. Model a raised matte-rubber tail button as its own submesh. Matte the crenellations (metal about 0.3, smoothness 0.3 or less). Note: the round 5 rebuild gave the recessed switch's 166 faces the dark knurl texel (r5_viewmodel hand-in), and that is what turned the recess into a hole.
- **[major] intro_trex_sheet.jpg (f_12-f_26)** (creatures: T. rex roar (opening hero shot)): The roar is a rubber-suit Godzilla pose. The head drops to the grass for about 0.6 s (f_13-f_15), snaps to shoulder height in one frame (≤0.21 s, about 170 px, f_15 to f_16), then the torso rears to about 60° (f_17-f_24; f_19 is upright with the head thrown back) while the tail stays low and level. The roar goes up and to screen right in near-profile, not at the glass and not at the ~53° three-quarter view that IntroTrexFaces aims for.
  - Fix: Re-author Roar_Massive as the critic says, keeping bone and clip names. Anticipation of 0.4-0.6 s with the head no lower than the hips, a 25-35° forward-and-up thrust with the spine within about 15° of level, the tail lifting as a counterweight, a head shake, then a settle. The whole roar f_12-f_26 lasts about 3.0 s at 0.213 s per frame, which matches the 3.2 s clip starting at the cut, so the head-down 'grazing' is most likely the clip's own anticipation: re-keying fixes it and blending alone will not. Also raise the TriggerRoar transition from 0.14 s (both AC_Tyrannosaurus controllers) to 0.3-0.4 s. Make the hold-and-look reach IntroTrexFaces before the cut by starting it earlier.
- **[major] trex_reveal_sheet.jpg (f_07-f_11)** (creatures: T. rex turn rate): The T. rex swings 90° in about 0.4-0.6 s of game time (about 150-225°/s), from a narrow front-on silhouette to a full walking profile, at the moment the player is meant to stare at it. A 7-ton animal turns like a raptor.
  - Fix: Use the critic's rates for Patrol and Scripted: 45-60°/s walking, 30-40°/s standing. Drop the 1.25-1.45x multipliers for species without turn clips (DinoControllerBase.cs lines 719/743/747, TrexAI.cs 777/781; base 135°/s at DinoConfig.cs line 441). Keep Hunt and Attack faster (about 70-90°/s) so the chase stays fair; round 5 already floors the heading factor at 0.5 there. Best: author 90° turn clips for the T. rex.

- **[minor] trex_walk_side_sheet.jpg (also intro_trex f_05, f_26-f_29)** (creatures: T. rex jaw): The jaw hangs open about 25-30° with teeth bared in every walking and holding frame, so the head reads as an animatronic stuck open and the roar has nothing to open from.
  - Fix: Key the jaw closed, or parted 3-6°, in Walk_Stomp and Idle_Heavy. ApplyJawArticulation can only add opening (targetDeg = max(0, open - animOpen)), so it needs a close term or new clip keys. Open the jaw through TriggerJawPulse for snorts and the roar.
- **[minor] hand_idle_sheet.jpg (24 frames)** (first-person hand: idle): The idle is one regular sine: the tail cap moves +1 to -5 to +1 px with a period of exactly 10 frames (5.0 s, 0.2 Hz) and no other drift. It is subtle but mechanical when the player stands still. Small correction to the critic: the sideways motion reaches 2 px, in step with the vertical one, not 1 px.
  - Fix: Add a slow Perlin drift (0.05-0.12 Hz, 1-2 mm, 0.3-0.5° yaw and roll), vary the breath period by ±15 %, and add a weight shift every 6-10 s.
