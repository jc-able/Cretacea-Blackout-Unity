# Review 4 (Nov 20, final state) — segment `interiors` (first critic 7/10, second critic 7/10)

Visitor Center interiors, power on and blackout (Play mode, HUD on)

## Verdict

This is a clear step up from review 3. The blackout half now looks like a shipped horror game: neutral grey darkness, warm battery islands, small red pools, and a beam whose pool leads the eye and shows texture. Four of review 3's seven majors are fixed outright (ceiling fan, white-tile clip, red flood, battery 'eyes'), the lobby is no longer sepia and the placeholder props are nearly all gone. It is not amazing yet because the power-on half still reads as clean, sparse sets: the break room is unchanged since review 3 and still shows the tan carton fridge, and the gift shop is understocked and lit only from its edges. Some polish items also remain: olive CCTV feeds, the stair-stepped office corner and the lobby beam shaft.

## Strengths

- Review 3 major FIXED (torch lighting the ceiling): the wall pool is now the brightest thing in every blackout room. Wall pool against the brightest ceiling block: 147/86 in records_dark (review 3: 144/161), 168/95 in office_dark, 132/75 in breakroom_dark. The brightest 32 px blocks all sit on the wall pool.
- Review 3 major FIXED (white-tile clip): in feedprep_white_tile, pixels at 250 or above fell from 1,321 to 128 (12_ch3: 1,364 to 136) and the 40x30 core from 242 to 210. Tile grout and the paper notice read in the centre of the spot.
- Review 3 major FIXED (red flood): in corridor_dark, red-dominant pixels fell from 20.7 % to 0.6 % and the near right wall is neutral (R/G 2.29 to 1.05). The emergency head is a deep red lens (max luma 83, no pink-white core) over a small scallop.
- Review 3 major FIXED (battery unit 'two eyes'): the unit now shows a grey housing with a dark front band, a green charge LED and dark head cans, readable with the beam off and on.
- The blackout reads as a real blackout. office_dark measures R/G 1.01 and B/G 1.00. With the flashlight off, unlit walls sit inside the target: corridor right wall 15 to 19, office exit wall 10 to 17.5. The glove now holds 30-34/255 instead of a black cut-out.
- Placeholders replaced: the boots and duffel are built from several parts, the towel is folded, the fiche reader has a lit screen, the gift stock is printed and the feed tubs are labelled. The first room gained a beige 90s keyboard, a phone, a logbook, a balanced-arm lamp and a handwritten night-shift whiteboard.
- lobby_dark (moonlit roof-grid shadows, warm bone under the beam), records_dark, the staff-exit collapse with rain through the hole, and corridor_dark_noflash are genuinely good horror frames. Colour in the lit rooms is natural and consistent (sRGB R/G 1.11-1.12, B/G 0.87-0.89).
- Review 3 minors fixed: the floating plant-leaf shadow in office_on, the torch pickup glowing on the desk in the blackout, and the blank grey panel in the corridor (now a cork board with notices).

## Dropped (4)

- Finding 7 (lobby_on, minor): the lit lobby lacks a cool moonlit counterpoint; raise MoonFill until the floor reads B/G 0.95-1.0. — Review 3's lobby-colour major is met. The frame now reads R/G 1.149 / B/G 0.878, the same as the other lit rooms (1.11-1.12 / 0.87-0.89) and on review 3's 1.15 target. With 9,500 cd mains and 4,200 cd uplights, moonlight at 8-14 lux could not cast visible roof-grid shadows, so the ask has no physical basis. The blue roof glass already gives the cool counterpoint. This is a taste call, not a flaw a player would see.
- Finding 9, part: the gift shop's right 'blank dark-green board' (x 1230-1280, y 270-360). — At 2x zoom the back wall ends at x 1228 and a dark recess or doorway lies beyond it. It is not a sign plate.
- Finding 1, part: 'every lit room shares the same floor tile, drop ceiling and beige paint, so no room has an identity'. — Overstated. The corridor has polished concrete, cream and blue-grey two-tone walls and pipes. The lobby has board-form concrete, polished stone and a glass roof. The gift shop has a smooth grey floor and a green counter. Only the office, lockers, break room and control room share the tile, the drop ceiling and the paint. The major stands on the gift shop and break room dressing.
- Finding 6, part: 'the bright vertical streak on the support pole' pulls the eye. — The crosshair (x 640, y 358) is on the pole, so the streak is the beam's hot spot on the surface the player aims at. That is correct behaviour. Only the shaft in the air is a fault.
