# Review 4 (Nov 20, final state) — segment `night_b` (first critic 7/10, second critic 7/10)

Substation 3, Old Maintenance Yard, Exit E-0, the coast road and the dawn ending

## Verdict

This round is clearly better than review 3, and the climax now works. E-0 is a real barred gate: it reads as closed from across the yard, and when it opens the leaves swing out and its signal turns green. The stripes are gone from the glove, and the amber, cool-white, green and red lamps tell one consistent story from the substation to the coast. It does not look amazing yet. The new hand brought a clipped white glare onto the wet floor under the torch (19, 23, 27). The ending's crane shot is still a carpet of saturated tree crowns with no mist. And the dry substation slab, the sea that never shows from the coast road, the big red lever that never moves and the boat with no wake are all still there.

## Strengths

- Review 3 majors. E-0 gate does not read as closed: FIXED. Hazard stripes printed on the glove: FIXED (25 and 26 show a clean glove and torch). Ending crane shot looks like a diorama: PARTLY FIXED, still a major below. Review 3 minors. Bollard collar clipping: FIXED. Ending caption inside the picture and boat boxed in by the rail: FIXED. Transfer lever does not move: PARTLY FIXED. Taupe dawn clouds: PARTLY FIXED. Matte substation slab, no sea from the coast road, boat with no wake: STILL THERE.
- E-0 is now the hero it should be (25). A welded palisade with stiles, a diagonal brace, a solid kick plate with hazard stripes, red signal heads, a crisp green E-0 SERVICE EXIT lightbox and its green downlight. From the yard (24, yard_before_strike, yard_strike, free-camera yard) it reads as a lit, barred screen with two red lamps, not an open doorway. Frame 26 sells the payoff: the leaves are swung out, the signal shows green, and the wet coast road with its sodium reflections lies beyond.

Green for the escape: bollards, E-0 and the assembly sign. Red for the gate signals and the coast flare. The floods are not blown out (heads average 161 and 172, max 242, no clipping) and the rain lights up inside their beams (21).
- The route bollard collar now reads as a saturated green band: mean 94/170/107, 0 % clipped in all three channels (review 3: 40 %). The green line reads from the near bollard to the vanishing point (22).
- Lightning and flashlight-off play both work. In yard_strike the bolt has branches, the cloud deck lifts and the asphalt, lane paint, pallets and containers keep their texture. In yard_before_strike the tree silhouettes, container colours and the lit gate still read. Route frames keep their 1st percentile at 7-11/255.
- Wet surfaces sell the storm: lamp streaks and reflections on the far substation slab (19), the yard-entry slab (23), the road beyond E-0 (26) and the coast road (27).
- The props at the payoff are believable: the breaker cabinet with breakers and a green LED column, the transfer box with gauge and hazard stripe, the containers, pallets and vehicles. The new knife switch visibly swings from A to B between 20 and 21. The new hand reads as a real gloved hand on a black torch, lit by its fill instead of a black cut-out.
- Ending shots 1 and 3 are genuinely pretty. Crisp horizon, gold-lit cumulus, a bright sun path, and teal water in the final shot (65/78/69). The warehouse now has a pitched sheet roof with skylights, vents and gutters. The storm free-camera yard got more readable: median 26 (review 3: 20), 14.5 % under 16/255 (review 3: 35 %).

## Dropped (6)

- Finding 1's supporting numbers: the strip means (106/111/128 against ~30 in review 3) and 'the same strip also jumps in 13 (86, was 27) and 15'. — The new viewmodel's lighter, lit glove sits inside that strip. 13's strip rises to 87 with no pixel above 200 (max 164), so that jump is the glove, not glare. The clipped share and the unchanged floor beside the hand are the valid evidence. The finding itself is kept.
- Finding 7 sub-claim: 'the white line right of the stern is a wave crest', i.e. the boat has no wake at all. — The streak sits at the same place behind the stern in all four captures (r3 and r4, ending 1 and 3) while the waves around it differ. It is the trail LookTerrainCoast.WakeFoam paints into the shore-foam water decal. What is really missing is the bow wave, the wake arms, hull foam and a bob.
- Finding 10 diagnosis: the streak comes from 'a lamp or a reflection probe ... never in view', so test by toggling the AuxYard lamps and re-capturing the probe. — The streak's mid-tones are cool-neutral (119/124/125), while every yard lamp is 2400-3300 K amber. It appears with the torch off and in free-camera frames. It shows only in east-facing wet views (the yard toward E-0, the coast), at the moon's angle below the horizon. AtmosphereController puts the storm moon at 19 lux, elevation 29°, azimuth 115°, angular diameter 0.9°, explicitly 'low ahead of the player's eastward route: the wet ground glints back at the camera'. In a storm with no visible moon a hard clipped glint is incoherent. The finding is kept with this cause.
- Finding 8 sub-claim: the sun is a defect, 'a clipped cream blob ... every pixel has a channel at 250 or above, with no disc edge'. — The numbers are right, but only ~100 px have all three channels clipped. The rest is R/G at 255 with B ~225: a soft cream glow with a small white core that reads as a low sun in haze. It is not what makes the shot look off; the warm cloud undersides and the yellow horizon are.
- Finding 4 sub-claim: 'The same in 26' (the frame lost ~0.7 stop since review 3). — 26 is unchanged from review 3 (median luma 20.6 vs 22.4; left wall 10/12/15 vs 10/12/16). Only 25 darkened.
- Finding 2 fix details: 'use the height falloff so the sea stays clear', and a local fog band to give the island aerial perspective. — The sea sits at the fog's base height, where height fog is densest, so raising fogMaxHeight also hazes the sea horizon (which is fine at dawn). A LocalVolumetricFog only renders inside the volumetric range (dawn.fogDepthExtent 250 m): it can band the valley under the crane but cannot haze the far ridge. That has to come from the global fog or the sky aerosols.
