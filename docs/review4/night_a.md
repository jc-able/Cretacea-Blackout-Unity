# Review 4 (Nov 20, final state) — segment `night_a` (first critic 7/10, second critic 7/10)

Night outdoors: plaza, loading dock, service paths, Tyrant Valley

## Verdict

Clearly better than review 3. The service path (14-16), the calm plaza and now the T. rex reveal look like a shipped game, and no frame is crushed or blown out. It is not amazing yet: with the light off, as the objective asks, Tyrant Valley is still a dim band from 8 to 63 out of 255 whose only highlights are a two-pixel, rose-coloured beacon and one flasher; the road the player walks still looks dry under the torch, with no rain hitting it; and a strike from behind turns the valley flat grey-blue. Review 3's majors: missing T. rex fixed, black loading dock fixed, pale bare walls in 15 fixed, dry valley road partly fixed, valley murk between strikes partly fixed.

## Strengths

- Review 3 major fixed: 18 finally shows the T. rex. A clean silhouette about 140 px long (correct scale at ~46 m) crosses the open gap down Service Road 3 against the strike-lit sky (low sky 103/255), with the beat's subtitles on screen.
- Review 3 major fixed: the loading dock (13) is readable. Pixels under 16/255 dropped from 54-60 % to about 23 % and the median rose from 13 to 34. The rust canopy is now grey corrugated metal, the floor is dry concrete, the battery unit is a visible twin-head fixture, and the truck, pallets, drums, jerry can and lit SERVICE ROAD 3 sign all read.
- Review 3 major fixed: the service-path walls in 15 are darker (left/back/right 42/62/29, was 60/89/51) with runoff streaks, a stencilled pump-room door, a PADDOCK 4 arrow and ferns over the coping. With the trench drain, the mirror-wet lane, the rain-ringed puddle at the gate (16), warm bulkheads and one cool fault flood, 14-16 are the best frames in the segment.
- The valley now has a lighting language that leads the player: amber delineators and chevrons that answer the torch, the red obstruction mast at Substation 3, bridge flashers and the overturned jeep's hazard glow. With the torch on (17) the frame has real highlights (p99.9 173).
- Silhouettes now read with the light off: the low storm sky behind the valley treeline is 44/255 (review 3: 34) against crowns at 19-22, and the road (29) stands out from the grass (17).
- The blackout plaza capture now shows the real blackout. The cobra head and lobby are dark, the palms, lamp post, bench, bin and building read as silhouettes, and nothing is crushed (p1 6, 2.9 % under 8/255).
- NightCalm_plaza_eye is still a hero frame: a sodium pool, the lit lobby, a legible protocol sign, and the jungle hill under broken cloud.
- Rain reads as rain in every frame: slanted streaks lit by lamps and the beam, and the free-camera seams are gone. There is no banding, and no frame is crushed (p1 of 6 or more everywhere).
- Grass under a flash is less teal than in review 3 (hue 155-159, was 169-181). The new glove and torch replace the mitten, and review 3's stair-step shadow on the sleeve is gone.

## Dropped (3)

- #10 NightCalm_plaza_eye: the cobra-head lens is a flat, clipped card — A lit lens clipping at night is how real lamps photograph and how shipped games draw them. The head is a detailed model above a warm pool with wet reflections, and at 1x it reads as a lamp, not a card. No player would notice, and the frame is still the hero shot. Its clipped area did grow from 24 % to 48 %; that is worth a glance at DiffuserNits 25, but it is not a finding.
- #15 15_ch3_path_raptor_crossing: the empty junction — This is capture timing, not a game flaw. The shot comes after the roughly 1-second crossing, as review 3's check established, and in the game the player sees the raptor. The crossing is now recorded as slowed-time frames by Tools/lookdev/motion_set.sh (commit c0f2e42, review4/motion), which is where it should be judged.
- #3's cause: 'a strike from behind the player' (strike azimuth 250, view yaw 78), and the fix 'bias random strikes into the forward 140 degrees' as the cure for this frame — capture_set.sh line 84 calls HoldLightningForCapture(1f, 250f, 50f), which sets the flash light's forward to yaw 250. The light therefore comes from azimuth 70, 8 degrees left of the view: the strike is ahead of the camera, exactly as in review 3, which used the same line. LightningBoltController.OnStrike puts the glow at -light.forward (ahead) and draws a bolt only when Random.value < 0.55. This hold rolled no bolt, and the 1.2 s wait let the exposure adapt. That is why the frame is flatter than review 3's. Biasing strikes forward would not change this frame; I kept the symptom with a different fix.
