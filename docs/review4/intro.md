# Review 4 (Nov 20, final state) — segment `intro` (first critic 7.5/10, second critic 7/10)

Opening cinematic (Play mode frames, in order, about 2.4 s apart)

## Verdict

Much better, and the day half is close to the AAA bar, but it is not amazing yet. All three of review 3's creature majors are fixed: the rex's face is whole inside its pane in 13, the trikes in 09 read as three animals, and the herd is gone from the closing aerial. The plaza now glows golden, the sunset sky and storm deck appear in 14-15, and the night plaza has real warm pools. Two things still stop it. First, the backlit hills of the 'CLOSING TIME' aerial (14-15) are sprinkled with glittering specks. Second, the first playable frame (19-20) is only partly fixed: the props are real now, but the room is sparse, mostly floor, evenly lit, with dark monitors and no horror mood. The rest is polish: grey clouds in the sauropod shot, invisible glass and rain, cloned pale trunks and a 'face' on the water cooler. Note that the caption list is shifted: 07 is still the plaza, 09 the sauropod rail, 13 the roar, 14 the 'CLOSING TIME' card and 15 the sunset aerial.

## Strengths

- Exposure from day through dusk to night is controlled, with no black or white frame at any cut. Day frames 01-13 clip at most 0.09 %; only the sun glow in 14-15 clips. The darkest 1 % stays at or above 8/255 in every frame, and the night frames keep their detail (16-18: p1 9-13).
- The golden afternoon finally reads golden. In 05 and 07 long, low shadows rake the plaza, the paving is warm (frame R/B 1.20-1.24) and the cumulus have warm edges. The opening's clouds have gone from cool (hue 195) to warm (hue 50) (review 3's 'white mid-afternoon' minor is fixed in the plaza shots).
- Review 3's creature majors are fixed. In 13 the rex's head is whole and sunlit inside the middle pane. In 09 the three trikes stand apart at three depths, and the brachiosaurus's tail ends 85 px clear of them. No animal is left in the closing aerial (StoryDirector despawns them at shot 5).
- The T. rex beat works: in 11 a dozen visitors stand as silhouettes at the glass, and in 13 a 3/4 head roars with its jaw open, framed by two mullions with visitors' heads in the bottom corners.
- Shot 5 now shows the sky: the sun sinks under a dark storm deck, amber last light catches the hill and the empty plaza is in teal shade (15), as the bible describes DuskTransition. The 'CLOSING TIME' card sits over warm sunset cumulus (14). Review 3's 'no sky' minor is fixed.
- The night aerial (16) has real warm pools: amber (hue 25-26) at about four times the brightness of the paving, so 'warm lamps in the night' finally reads (review 3's 'mauve sheet' minor is fixed).
- The office props are real now: a mesh chair, binders, a walkie, a balanced-arm lamp, a mug, a handwritten duty roster and live CCTV feeds. The torch is visible and lit on the desk, and the thunder caption no longer covers it. The troffers are out of the first frame and the stepped shadow bands on the right wall are gone.
- All four title cards (01, 14, 17, 19) share one lower-left plate with an amber edge. The crowd (families, a ranger, a queue at the rail, children walking) makes the park read open. The terracotta slabs at the pond are now a grey boulder and a mossy log.

## Dropped (5)

- Finding 5, intro_15: the 'mustard haze band' as flat fill, and the fix that raises dusk fogMeanFreePath from 420 to 900 m and lowers anisotropy from 0.7 to 0.55. — The band is not flat (std 40). It is a graded sun-shaft haze over the canopy and reads as the intended golden storm-front mist, the most atmospheric part of the shot. Thinning the fog would remove it. The real dead area is the meadow under it, which is kept.
- Finding 10, intro_17: the apron puddle 'reads as a dark stain' because it mirrors neither the window nor the lamps. — It is physically right. From this lens (2-3 m up, about 10 m out), the window's mirror image meets the ground about 3.8 m from the wall and the lamps' about 5 m out, beyond a puddle hugging the apron. A puddle mirroring the dark wall and sky is darker than the lit matte concrete around it, and that reads as wet.
- Finding 6, intro_16: 'the blue hour ... is never on screen'. — The NightCalm blend runs during the 4.5 s of shot 5b, between the two samples, so stills 2.4 s apart cannot prove it is absent. What the frames and the code do show is that it is very short; that part is kept as 'too fast'.
- Finding 2: the 23:05 card frame (19) shows the same clock, shade and monitor stack. — In 19 the shade hangs clear, right of the bank (x 755-822), because the camera is still blending in (see missed). The stack exists only in 20, the settled start view.
- Finding 9, intro_17: 'no streaks appear in the three wall-pack cones even at 3x gain'. — At 3x gain a handful of faint streaks are visible in the cones. The rain exists but is far too sparse and dim to read at 1x, so the finding stands with this correction.
