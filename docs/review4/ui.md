# Review 4 (Nov 20, final state) — segment `ui` (first critic 8.5/10, second critic 8.5/10)

HUD, menus, journal, document reader, end screen

## Verdict

The interface itself is close to amazing. It is one warm, branded, legible system: every review-3 minor is fixed, the stencil titles, park badge and screwed steel cards finally give the menus a 1996 park-security identity, and keycaps, subtitles and objective copy read cleanly at 720p and tell a new player where to go. Review 3's only major for this segment, the end-screen BLACKOUT wordmark, is partly fixed: it measures 1.4:1 against the sky instead of 1.0:1, so it reads by colour but is not yet at the 3:1 asked for. What stops this set looking amazing is not the UI. The 'blackout corridor' HUD frame, and every menu shot over it, shows the corridor flat-lit and brighter than with the power on, most likely daylight state left over from the DawnCoast shots taken 10 s earlier, so the HUD has not actually been seen over the real blackout this round.

## Strengths

- One warm amber/cream system across HUD, pause, settings, controls, tablet, reader and end screen, now with a period identity: Stardos Stencil card titles, the park badge, steel cards with a bevel, corner screws and a faint wear grain. The cards sit at 13-18/255 and are no longer lifted.
- Review 3's minors are all fixed. There is one keycap style with 12 px legends on every keyboard key (SHIFT, C, E and F included) and subtle opaque row bands. The F chip lamp is a centred dot in a soft halo. The TAB hint has an even 12 px of padding in both the one-line (02) and two-line (06) banners. Transcript items are 27 px apart against an 18-19 px line pitch. YOU and STAFF CORRIDOR no longer collide, and LOADING DOCK is clear of its wall. Known documents are named, with one NOT FOUND wording. The tablet footer says how SYSTEMS unlocks.
- Over dark scenes the HUD is excellent: objective copy 14-15:1, amber header 9:1, subtitles 15.7:1 with a 9.5:1 amber speaker tag and two balanced lines.
- The objective copy works for a new player. Every line names the place plus a landmark you can see ('past the Control Room', 'beside the Gift Shop'), the tablet repeats it with a beacon on the map, and the long route after the manual is given one leg at a time.
- Focused buttons read 8:1 on the amber fill and idle ones 15:1. Settings rows, values, sliders and toggles are clean, aligned and consistent.
- The reader's paper prop sells the world (letterhead, typewriter face, CONTROLLED COPY stamp, stains, punch holes), with a matching transcript beside it.
- The end screen works as a payoff: the badge and wordmark sit over a soft pool, the stats sit on a card low on the sea, the buttons are clear, and CRETACEA now measures 3.4:1 plus its shadow.

## Dropped (1)

- Finding 8: the Xbox face buttons on controls.png are grey discs with no colour cue, so B (crouch) and X (interact) look alike. — This is a taste call, not a flaw. Monochrome face-button glyphs are a standard option: Microsoft ships monochrome glyph sets, and many AAA games use them. The letters measure 11 px, the same as the HUD chips. A, B, X and Y are distinct letterforms, and nothing on the page is misread. Four saturated button colours would also fight the warm one-accent palette the look bible asks for.
