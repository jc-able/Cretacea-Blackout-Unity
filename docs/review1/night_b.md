# Review 1 (Nov 20, before the lead's look fixes in 07fd73d) — segment night_b

Images: Screenshots/review1/ (same camera positions as Tools/lookdev/capture_set.sh and route_test.py).

### Critic segment `night_b` (score 4/10)

Not amazing yet. The night colour and lighting logic is now right: a blue storm, warm practical lamps as islands of light, amber at service points, green for the escape, controlled exposure and a good flashlight. But a player would spot several faults in the climax at once: chain-link fences that turn into blotchy tan walls, rain that reads as snow, hard-edged mirror rectangles on the ground, a shredded E-0 sign, neon-green emissives and glowing rods on the floor. The ending has a lovely sky and sun glint, but its first shot is overexposed, the crane shot shows the yard as a blockout, and the last image is a rescue boat built from boxes sitting on a white slab.

- **[blocker] 28_ending_3.png** (geometry): The last shot of the game frames the Coast Guard rescue boat (centre bottom, x≈515-630, y≈535-630). It is a stack of bare boxes: a box hull, a stepped box deckhouse and a stick mast, sitting on a flat light-grey slab that sticks out of its side like a plank. It reads as a blockout toy and kills the payoff.
  - Likely cause: Zone4_TerrainJungleAndDawnCoastBuilder.BuildDawnCoastAndOceanVista builds 'DistantEvacBeacon_Bay' only from CreateBox (EvacHull_Main 22x3.2x7.2 DarkPaintedMetal, EvacDeckhouse/EvacBridgeHouse PlasterClean, EvacRadarMast...). The wake is 'EvacWakeFoam', a 32x0.18x9.6 m PlasterClean box. There is no boat model in Assets/Art.
  - Suggested fix: Delete the EvacWakeFoam slab and use the water's own foam for the wake (a foam generator or water decal on IslaTormenta_Sea). Until a real hull exists, push the boat 1.5-2 km out into dawn haze so only a silhouette and its beacon read, or cut it and end on the sun glint. Re-aim finalSeaCam in CameraDirector.PlayDawnCoastCraneShot so the boxes are never framed up close.
- **[major] 20_ch5_generator_running.png** (materials): Chain-link fences render as solid panels. In 20/21 the substation's 4.6 m fences fill the whole background as opaque tan panels with a dot texture and brown blotches; in 19 the same fences look like blank buildings and a concrete wall. In 27 only the span next to the camera is see-through and every span beyond is a solid grey wall; in 26 the view through the open gate is a grey wall. The substation looks walled in cheap plywood, and the coast road becomes a concrete chute that hides the sea.
  - Likely cause: WorldMaterialPalette.BuildChainlinkMaterial calls KeepAlphaCoverage(...,0.4f), which turns on mipMapsPreserveCoverage for Assets/Art/Decals/chainlink_albedo.png (28.9 % coverage at mip 0). I measured the texture: from mip 4 down, alpha is a uniform 0.24 everywhere, so the coverage scaler can only lift every texel over the 0.4 cutoff. Beyond about 5-10 m (sooner at grazing angles) the fence closes into a solid panel showing the albedo's rust spots.
  - Suggested fix: Turn preserve-coverage off for the chain-link texture, or author its mips so alpha at mip 3 and below stays under the cutoff. Distant fence fabric then thins out to see-through like a real fence, and the posts and rails carry the silhouette. Optionally add a distance LOD that is a transparent panel at about 15 % opacity.
- **[major] 22_ch5_green_route.png** (weather): The rain reads as snow in every storm frame (19, 22, 23, 24, 26, 27). It is short, fat, bright-white dashes and dots, all one size, spread evenly over the whole frame, including the dark sky far from any lamp. The tropical storm looks like a winter night; 22 and 27 are the worst.
  - Likely cause: StormRainOcclusionController.ConfigureParticleSystems: velocityScale 0.022 gives streaks of about 0.2 m; startSize 0.012-0.02 with minParticleSize 0.0026 keeps them at least 2 px wide. MAT_RainStreaks.mat has _Color alpha 0.7, _MaxNits 70 (about 5x scene white at storm EV) and _SkyLight 1, so every streak is lit by the moon/sky plus the flashlight and all of them come out white.
  - Suggested fix: Make the streaks longer, thinner and fainter: velocityScale about 0.05-0.06, startSize 0.006-0.01, alpha about 0.3, _MaxNits about 20, _SkyLight about 0.3, so only lamp and beam cones show bright streaks. Add a distant rain-sheet layer (scrolling streak cards or local fog with streak noise) for depth, as TLOU and RE7 do.
- **[major] 19_ch5_substation_dark.png** (materials): Flat, light-grey rectangles with hard edges lie on the ground: two parallelograms right of the hand in 19 (x≈810-1020, y≈465-530) and one on the road in 23 (x≈860-1000, y≈450-472). They read as primitive quads stuck to the floor.
  - Likely cause: The puddle decals' gloss and normals ignore the puddle shape. LookWeather.ConfigurePuddleMaterial sets _MaskBlendSrc = 1 and _NormalBlendSrc = 1 on MAT_WetPuddleDecal_Atlas with no mask map. In HDRP's DecalData.hlsl, 1 means 'mask map blue x fade', which is 1 everywhere, so smoothness 0.96 and the ripple normal cover the whole projector box. Only the albedo follows the atlas alpha. The lightning emission set by WetnessDriver.UpdatePuddleFlash is not masked either, because there is no _EmissiveColorMap. This affects all puddles game-wide.
  - Suggested fix: Set _MaskBlendSrc and _NormalBlendSrc to 0 (base-colour map alpha) so gloss and normals follow the irregular puddle shape. Mask the flash emission with an RGB copy of the atlas alpha as _EmissiveColorMap, or turn _AffectEmission off.
- **[major] 28_ending_1.png** (exposure): The first ending shot is overexposed. 63 % of the right half of the sky has a clipped channel (19 % of the whole frame); the clouds near the sun dissolve into flat yellow-white and the sun glint is a white bar. 28_ending_3, a later shot with the sun in frame, clips only 0.1 %, so the problem is timing, not the scene.
  - Likely cause: StoryDirector.DawnCoastEndingRoutine cross-fades storm to dawn over 8.5 s (atmosphere.SetPreset(DawnCoast, 8.5f)), but the first glide lasts 5.5 s. The histogram exposure (dawn EV 10-13, adaptDarkToLight 2.4) is still climbing from the night value while the shot plays.
  - Suggested fix: Make the storm-to-dawn change under a short fade to black or the title card, with SetPreset(DawnCoast, 0), and hold about 1 s. Or lock the cutscene to fixed exposure at the EV of ending_3 for the first glide and release it after.
- **[major] 25_ch6_e0_gate_closed.png** (ui): The E-0 exit sign, the target of the whole climax, is shredded. 'SERVICE EXIT' is an unreadable spray of green pixels, and 'E-0' and the arrow are broken into fragments with holes (top centre, x≈470-620, y≈95-175; same in 26).
  - Likely cause: LookSigns.SignMaterial, and the builder's MAT_SignE0, use the spray-stencil artwork e0_exit_decal.png with _AlphaCutoff 0.5 and a flat green emission. About a third of that artwork's letter coverage is semi-transparent spray edge (alpha 16-239), so the cutoff eats the strokes. Lower mips at distance make it worse.
  - Suggested fix: Use the clean e0_exit.png artwork (solid letters), or a lightbox texture made for emission, for the back-lit header. If the stencil look is wanted, drop the cutoff to about 0.15 and use the alpha as an emission mask. Keep the letters just above scene white so they read instead of blooming.
- **[major] 22_ch5_green_route.png** (lighting): The escape-green emissives clip to pure neon: the bollard caps (22, right edge: about 3,900 px with G ≥ 250 and R < 120), the green conduit (21), and the E-0 lamp and letters (25/26). They bloom into flat green pucks and throw pure-green pools on the gravel that look radioactive. The nearest bollard also shows two stacked green discs.
  - Likely cause: The palette's EmissiveGreen (0.22, 1.0, 0.42; linear 0.04, 1.0, 0.15) is almost a pure primary, at 22 nits (WorldMaterialPalette.cs line 149). The custom per-channel tonemap keeps that hue at the clip, and the bloom threshold is 0. Zone3 BuildEastOnlyAuxRouteBeacons also adds an 'AuxGreenCap' cylinder primitive in EmissiveGreen on top of the collar lens that LookFixtures already adds.
  - Suggested fix: Desaturate the escape green (for example sRGB 0.35, 1.0, 0.6) and lower the lens nits so the core sits just above scene white at storm EV (about 15-20 nits). Delete the AuxGreenCap primitive and keep one lens per bollard. Soften the Light_AuxRouteGreen ground pools (fewer candela, a slightly cyan-green tint) so they read as light, not paint.
- **[major] 21_ch5_aux_power_floods.png** (geometry): Glowing rods on the floor. A straight red emissive rod runs the full width of 19 (y≈540) like a laser tripwire. In 20/21 the same kind of rod lies diagonally across the pad and becomes a neon-green tube after power-up. Real conduits do not glow, the rigid straight rods read as primitives, and a red line on the floor breaks the 'red only as beacons' rule.
  - Likely cause: Zone3_SubstationAndYardBuilder.BuildSubstationInteractStation calls BuildConduitRun(parent, "Conduit_"+name, conduitPath, 0.032f, p.EmissiveRed) for every station, and SubstationStation.powerConduitRenderers switches it from red to green.
  - Suggested fix: Make the conduits plain steel or dark paint in their troughs. Show progress on the per-station status lamps and on a lit mimic panel at the switch cabinet (three breaker LEDs going red to green). If a floor cue must stay, use small, low-nit LED markers on the trough clamps, not a glowing tube.
- **[major] 27_escape_coast_road.png** (geometry): The run to the coast is a fenced concrete chute with nothing at the end: no sea, no coastline, no lamp or beacon, just a timber rail in the dark. The frame has almost no highlights (99th-percentile luminance 75/255). The far half of the road is a saturated blue band that starts abruptly about a third of the way down and reads as a blue tarp.
  - Likely cause: The fences render opaque (see the chain-link finding), which hides the coast. The blue band is the wet asphalt mirroring the sky. It starts where the 'CoastRoad' probe in LookProbes starts (centre X 545, 40 m long, so X 525-565), while the 42 m road runs X 505-547, so the near half gets a different, darker reflection. The evac boat's Light_EvacBeacon_White and Light_EvacSearchlight (VCExit group) cannot be seen from the road.
  - Suggested fix: Fix the chain-link mips so the jungle and the sea show through. Re-centre or extend the CoastRoad probe to cover X 503-549 so the reflection is continuous. Give the run a goal: the evac searchlight sweeping over the overlook at the end of the road, plus a warm lamp or flare at the rail.
- **[major] 28_ending_2.png** (geometry): The crane shot looking back at the island shows the Old Yard as a blockout. There is an empty flat slab, containers and a grey box as bare blocks, a workshop that is a yellow box with red trim, and a plain tan perimeter wall with a flat grey door across the bottom of the frame. The forest edge behind is a row of identical straight trunks. There is no mist, although the caption says 'misty island'.
  - Likely cause: In CameraDirector.PlayDawnCoastCraneShot, cranePeak = overlook + (-7, 14.2, 4) aimed at valleyCanopyLook (310, 14, -15) frames the yard from 15 m up. The dawn fog mean free path is 1500 m (LookAtmosphereProfiles), so no mist forms in the valley.
  - Suggested fix: Raise the crane and aim it over the canopy so the yard drops out of frame or is a sliver, and the hills and valley are the subject. Add low dawn mist over Tyrant Valley: a Local Volumetric Fog band about 5-25 m high, or a lower dawn fogMaxHeight with a shorter mean free path.
- **[major] 28_ending_3.png** (colour): The dawn misses the Look Bible's 'sun on the sea horizon, peach to pale-blue sky, teal water'. The sun stands well above the horizon, the sky around it is yellow-white, the backlit cumulus on the left are muddy khaki-brown with no bright rims, and the sea is grey-lavender with a thin dark seam along the horizon (ending 1 and 3).
  - Likely cause: AtmosphereController.GetKeyLook for DawnCoast: elevationDeg 9.5, temperatureK 5750, 115000 lux, which is a noon-strength neutral sun. Nothing cool lifts the cloud shade. The sea's ambientScattering is 0.15 (LookTerrainWater), so grazing views show only reflected sky.
  - Suggested fix: Sun elevation about 3-4° (let the physical sky redden it), about 40-60k lux, a higher cloudAmbientDimmer so the cloud shade goes blue-grey, and a peach horizonTint. Raise the sea's ambient and height scattering (0.15 → about 0.35) so the foreground water reads teal, and soften the horizon seam with a little dawn haze.
- **[major] 22_ch5_green_route.png** (materials): Wet ground sparkles like glitter. The gravel path in 22 is a bright, fine white speckle that, together with the white rain, reads as frost or snow. The wet asphalt in NightStormBlackout_coast_eye is a field of cyan-white sparkles around the rail posts instead of a smooth sheen.
  - Likely cause: WetnessDriver raises smoothness on GravelGround and AsphaltWet, whose normal maps have fine detail. The result is specular aliasing that TAA cannot resolve.
  - Suggested fix: Cap wet smoothness on gravel at about 0.6-0.7 (gravel drains). Reduce normal strength as wetness rises, since water fills the small surface relief. Turn on geometric specular AA on the outdoor Lit materials. Wet gravel should get darker, not brighter.
- **[minor] 20_ch5_generator_running.png** (lighting): The foreground lamp in 20/21 is a glowing orange 'faucet' on a bare black stick. The whole bulkhead housing glows and blooms (x≈405-465, y≈200-240). The 2.9 m post and the 8 m flood mast behind it are plain square boxes, and the flood head in 21 is a small white box with a halo.
  - Likely cause: LookFixtures.Bulkhead calls GlowModel(body, ..., 60f), so the entire lamp_bulkhead FBX glows at 60 nits. LookFixtures' own note measured that as a blob; 25 nits keeps the frame readable. BulkheadPost_* (0.14 m) and FloodMast_* (0.24 m) are CreateBox in Zone3_SubstationAndYardBuilder.
  - Suggested fix: Make only the glass emissive, at about 25 nits. Use round galvanised poles with a base plate and bracket for the posts and masts.
- **[minor] 28_ending_1.png** (materials): The cliff-top grass behind the rail stands on a mirror: a uniform gold specular sheet between the blades reflects the sun, as if the grass grew in water (x≈680-1210, y≈555-655).
  - Likely cause: The DawnCoast KeyLook keeps wetness = 0.5, so the terrain stays glossy, and at the low sun's grazing angle that becomes a mirror.
  - Suggested fix: Drop dawn wetness to about 0.2 on terrain (keep puddles on roads), or use the soil mask so ground under vegetation stays rough.
- **[minor] NightStormBlackout_yard_eye.png** (vegetation): The same umbrella-shaped tree is repeated at the same size along the yard wall (yard_eye, 24, and the big one in 22) and reads more like African savanna than tropical jungle. In 28_ending_2 the forest edge is a fence of identical straight trunks.
  - Likely cause: One jungle_tree prefab is scattered along the perimeter with little variation in scale and rotation.
  - Suggested fix: Mix palms, tree ferns and broadleaf canopy trees along the wall, vary scale by about ±30 % and lean, and break up the canopy line.
- **[minor] 25_ch6_e0_gate_closed.png** (lighting): A saturated orange wash covers the right fifth of the frame at E-0 (x≈960-1280, also in 26). It competes with the green exit signal and makes the frame two-tone.
  - Likely cause: Probably the E-0 control-box beacon (Light_E0ControlBox_Beacon: 420 cd, amber, 9 m range) on the wall beside the gate.
  - Suggested fix: Halve its intensity or range (about 200 cd or 5 m) so it stays a small amber accent and the E-0 green dominates.
- **[minor] 21_ch5_aux_power_floods.png** (story): The aux-power payoff and the T. rex break-in are undersold. The frame looks almost the same as 20: one flood head as a small dot, no beams in the rain. The caption 'THE T. REX SMASHES THROUGH THE FENCE' plays while the camera stares at the cabinet, with no danger cue in view.
  - Likely cause: Only one of the four Light_AuxSubFlood masts is visible from the lever, and the floods' volumetric dimmer of 1.0 shows no cones in the rain.
  - Suggested fix: Raise the floods' volumetric dimmer to about 1.6-2.0 so the cones read in the rain, and switch them on one after another. Add a short look-at, or a red beacon flash toward the fence break, when the T. rex arrives.
