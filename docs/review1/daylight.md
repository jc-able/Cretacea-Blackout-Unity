# Review 1 (Nov 20, before the lead's look fixes in 07fd73d) — segment daylight

Images: Screenshots/review1/ (same camera positions as Tools/lookdev/capture_set.sh and route_test.py).

### Critic segment `daylight` (score 4/10)

Not amazing yet. Daylight at eye level is a real step up. The sky is blue with volumetric cumulus, the grass is green, close-up materials are believable and exposure is sane, so the owner's 'sepia day' complaint is mostly answered there. But as soon as the sun is low or the camera rises, the look falls apart: the impostor forest turns every hill into yellow cardboard blocks; the dawn ending blows out to a lemon-white wash with 21% of pixels clipped, because the tone curve has only about 3.6 stops of highlight room; the dusk valley crushes to black; and the built world still reads as a kit (an empty 131 x 64 m plaza, a glass-shoebox Visitor Center, a checkerboard lawn, a river that reads as a dry ditch, a flat dark sea under an olive band).

- **[blocker] DuskTransition_plaza_eye.png** (vegetation): The jungle hill behind the Visitor Center is the largest thing in the frame, and it renders as a heap of flat mustard-yellow cardboard blocks (sample (125,108,65)). Each block is lit on one half and dark on the other, and yellow speckles dot the bare slope; there are no crowns, no leaves and no aerial haze. The same defect shows in DuskTransition_meadow_eye (a khaki crumpled-paper hill) and DawnCoast_valley_eye (the hill left of the path reads as stacked grey-tan stone). From above, in all three aerials, the trees read as tan paper cut-outs with trunk streaks, worst at the right edge of DawnCoast_aerial_sw. By day the same hills read as vertical streaks, which are the rasterised trunks.
  - Likely cause: In LookVegetationImpostors.cs SaveMesh, each crossed quad gets vertex normals of up*0.35 +/- side*0.9. The normal therefore sweeps sideways within the card's own plane, so a low sun (5.5 deg at dusk, 9.5 deg at dawn) lights one half fully and leaves the other dark, which makes the faceted blocks. SaveMaterial sets the cards to LitStandard with smoothness 0 and no foliage translucency, while the near trees are translucent foliage, so far trees come out brighter and yellower than near ones. ShadowCastingMode is Off, so crowns never shadow each other. The atlas has only front and side orthographic views and no top view, so from above you see card edges and long trunks.
  - Suggested fix: Derive a per-texel normal map from the depth buffer that Rasterise() already fills and bind it (as a minimum, give the whole card one up-and-outward normal). Use the foliage translucent material type with the same diffusion profile as the near trees, and a darker, slightly desaturated impostor tint so value and hue match the mesh trees. Bake ambient occlusion from that depth into the atlas (or let impostors cast cached shadows) so the canopy has depth. For hills seen from the plaza and from above, move to hemi-octahedral impostors with 8-12 views including a top view.
- **[blocker] DawnCoast_coast_eye.png** (exposure): The ending view is blown out: 21% of the frame and 38% of the sky clip to white. The sun is a white blob about 200 px wide that washes the right half of the sky lemon-yellow. The sun's reflection on the sea is a narrow, hard-edged rectangle. The sky is yellow and dull grey-blue (upper left (89,106,123)), not the bible's peach to pale blue, and bloom veils the whole frame. DawnCoast_valley_eye has the same problem: 17% clipped, and the far trees dissolve into glare.
  - Likely cause: LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs line 164: the shared custom curve uses shoulderLength 0.55 (f-stops added to the range). With toeLength 0.5 and shoulderStrength 0.75 it reaches white at an input of about 1.5, only about 3.6 stops above mid-grey (ACES keeps about 6.5). The channels clip one at a time, so an orange sky turns lemon. Dawn evMax 13 (line 257) leaves no room to stop down when the camera faces the 115,000 lux sun (AtmosphereController.cs line 126). Bloom intensity 0.18 with scatter 0.68 and threshold 0 (lines 168 and 271) spreads the clipped area. The sun sits at 9.5 deg (line 127), well above the horizon rather than on it.
  - Suggested fix: Raise shoulderLength from 0.55 to 2.5-3.0 for all presets (white point about 5-8), then re-tune evCompensation per preset. Dawn: evMax 13 -> 15, evCompensation -0.15 -> -0.5, bloomIntensity 0.18 -> 0.06, bloomScatter 0.68 -> 0.55. For a peach-to-pale-blue sky, set dawn horizonTint to about (1.0,0.84,0.76) and zenithTint to about (0.82,0.90,1.0). Lower the dawn sun from 9.5 to 3-4 deg. Add more swell (see the sea finding) so the glitter path widens and breaks into glints.
- **[major] DuskTransition_valley_eye.png** (exposure): The frame is crushed and clipped at once. The lower 40% (the path and both tall-grass fields) averages 0.03 sRGB with 69% of pixels near-black, so the ground the player walks on vanishes. At the same time 9% of the sky clips: the lit cloud at top centre is a yellow-white hole. DuskTransition_plaza_eye also clips 37% of its upper-right sky.
  - Likely cause: With only about 3.6 stops of highlight room (see the dawn finding), the centre-weighted histogram (40-88%, LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs line 368) exposes for the bright gap in the clouds. Dusk adds no fill: indirectDiffuse stays at 1.0 (line 171), the Overcast clouds dim the sky ambient, the toe is 0.15 and contrast is 14 (line 206).
  - Suggested fix: Fix the shoulder first. Then for dusk: evCompensation -0.2 -> +0.4, indirectDiffuse 1.0 -> 1.3, toeStrength 0.15 -> 0.05, and histogram high percentile 88 -> 80 (or mask-weighted metering) so a gap in the clouds does not set the exposure. Target: foreground grass at about 0.12-0.18 sRGB with visible blades, and under 1% of sky pixels clipped.
- **[major] GoldenDay_coast_eye.png** (colour): A flat olive-grey band (97,102,94) about 2 deg tall sits between the sea and the sky, so the sea never meets the horizon. The same cause makes the top third of every aerial (all three aerial_sw frames and GoldenDay_valley_air) a flat khaki or beige field, (138,142,125) by day and (151,134,105) at dawn, where the island and the sea should continue. It reads as smog and gives the aerials their sepia cast. In DawnCoast_coast_eye the sun's reflection stops with a hard edge at this band.
  - Likely cause: The free capture camera renders with a far clip of 1000 m (CretaceaCliTools.cs line 52 default), so the infinite sea and the island beyond 1 km are not drawn. What shows there is the Physically Based Sky's ground, tinted by groundTint (0.10,0.13,0.08) (LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs line 156) and fogged with FogColorMode.SkyColor (line 335), which samples that olive ground for every view below the horizon. In Play mode the player camera clips at 4000 m (CretaceaWorldAssembler.cs line 599). From the dawn overlook (eye about 43 m above the sea) the band shrinks to about 0.6 deg (about 7 px), but it is still a dark line on the ending's horizon.
  - Suggested fix: Recapture review frames with --far_clip 20000 (or at least the player's 4000) so they show what players see. In the game, raise the player camera's far clip to about 20 km (the sky and the infinite ocean are cheap), or thicken the distant fog so the sea fully fades before the far plane. Set groundTint per preset to a sea/haze colour (day about (0.32,0.44,0.55), dusk about (0.25,0.30,0.36), dawn about (0.55,0.52,0.50)) so nothing below the horizon goes olive.
- **[major] GoldenDay_plaza_eye.png** (colour): Sepia survives in the architecture: sunlit neutral greys turn orange-brown. The plaza paving reads (96,75,47), red/blue ratio 2.0, although the paving_stone texture is a mild warm grey (ratio 1.15). The yard perimeter wall reads (138,110,73) and the yard asphalt has a ratio of 1.9. The shade on the paving is brown (36,30,21), not the bible's blue. This orange floor fills half of GoldenDay_plaza_eye.
  - Likely cause: GoldenDay grade (LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs lines 178-190): toeStrength 0.28 and contrast 12 are applied per channel and widen colour ratios in the darks, because blue is crushed first. Saturation +8, white balance +4 and warm smhHighlights (1.04,1.0,0.95) stack on an already warm 27 deg sun. The MAT_Z1_PlazaPaving tint (1.05,1.02,0.98) (Zone1_VisitorCenterBuilder.cs line 1674) adds more.
  - Suggested fix: GoldenDay: temperature +4 -> -2, smhHighlights -> (1.01,1.0,0.99), toeStrength 0.28 -> 0.12, contrast 12 -> 6, saturation 8 -> 4, and let the sun's own colour carry the warmth. Paving tint -> (0.95,0.97,1.0), or switch to a grey granite paver. Tint ConcreteExterior slightly cool. Set smhShadows to (0.92,0.98,1.10) so shade reads blue. Check against a grey card: sunlit grey should read red/blue about 1.2-1.3, shade about 0.9.
- **[major] GoldenDay_coast_eye.png** (exposure): Day shade crushes to black. A third of the overlook foreground (the lower 40% of the frame) is near-black, and the shaded asphalt and posts lose all texture. The valley path in GoldenDay_valley_eye is (23,21,16) and reads as black tar. The shadow across the bottom of GoldenDay_yard_eye is near-black (16% of that region).
  - Likely cause: The same day grade: toeStrength 0.28 plus contrast 12 on the custom curve put the shade 1.5-2 stops darker than a camera would. The comment at LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs line 161 describes exactly this failure, for ACES.
  - Suggested fix: Day toeStrength 0.28 -> 0.10-0.12 and contrast 12 -> 6-8 (the same change as the colour finding). Shade on dark asphalt should sit near 0.12-0.15 sRGB and keep its texture.
- **[major] GoldenDay_yard_eye.png** (weather): Dirty clouds: a large, blurred olive-grey blob (77,82,69) hangs at the top right like a smudge on the lens, with no defined cumulus edge. The same blob appears at the top left of GoldenDay_plaza_eye and GoldenDay_meadow_eye. Shaded cloud sides elsewhere (cloud bases in GoldenDay_coast_eye, about (73,78,73)) are olive too, where cumulus bases should be blue-grey.
  - Likely cause: Two likely causes. First, cloud undersides take their ambient light from the lower hemisphere of the sky probe, which is the olive PBS ground (groundTint (0.10,0.13,0.08)). Second, the free capture camera uses SMAA and 6 warm-up frames (CretaceaCliTools.cs line 92; the warm-up count is capped at 10), while the clouds accumulate over many frames (temporalAccumulationFactor 0.95, LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs line 329), so near clouds are still low-res and soft.
  - Suggested fix: Check in Play mode with TAA first. Then lighten groundTint (see the horizon finding) and verify the cloud bases go blue-grey. If they are still mushy, use the Custom cloud preset with higher erosion for crisper bases. For review captures, use the player camera or allow more warm-up frames.
- **[major] GoldenDay_aerial_sw.png** (vegetation): The valley floor is a mown lawn with a checkerboard. The grass texture repeats as obvious light and dark squares across the meadows, very visible in all three aerials and in GoldenDay_valley_air. At eye level (GoldenDay_meadow_eye), Titan Meadows is flat lawn with sparse tufts and evenly scattered single palms, closer to a golf course than a lush tropical valley.
  - Likely cause: TL_Grass has a 7.5 m tile (LookTerrainLayers.cs line 35). The texture's own low-frequency light/dark variation repeats every 7.5 m, and grass carries almost all the weight in the lowland, so the 150 m and 50 m noise in Paint() does not break the pattern. Ground-cover details end at 55 m (LookVegetationDetails.cs line 227); beyond that only the bare texture shows.
  - Suggested fix: High-pass the large blotches out of tj_grass_BaseColor (derive_terrain_textures.py). Add a second grass variant (dry or lush) at about a 23 m tile, blended by the existing 50 m 'mid' noise. Push tall-grass and shrub clumps further out (detail distance 90 m with density falloff, or a cheap far-grass card layer). Plant understory bands (ferns, cycads, shrubs) and groups of trees along the meadow edges instead of single palms on open lawn.
- **[major] GoldenDay_coast_eye.png** (water): From the 43 m overlook the sea is a flat, featureless navy plane (30-60 sRGB). There are no visible swell lines, no whitecaps, and no reflected clouds or cloud-shadow patches, although the sky is full of cumulus. Just under the horizon the sea is (62,70,66) against a sky of about (140,140,135).
  - Likely cause: LookTerrainWater.BuildSea: largeWindSpeed 14 km/h (line 30) and ripplesWindSpeed 6 km/h (line 34) make a nearly calm sea, and simulationFoamAmount 0.35 (line 49) gives no whitecaps at that wind. The reflection of the bright horizon sky is weak.
  - Suggested fix: largeWindSpeed 14 -> about 35 km/h (a tropical trade-wind sea), ripplesWindSpeed 6 -> 12, simulationFoamAmount -> 0.5. Check the water's sky reflection at grazing angles, and check that volumetric cloud shadows reach the sea. The extra swell also gives the dawn glitter path its width.
- **[major] GoldenDay_plaza_eye.png** (geometry): The Main Plaza is an empty 131.5 x 64 m slab (about 8,400 square metres) with a handful of benches, bins and lamp posts. It reads as a parking lot, not a theme park. Two 0.4 m dark strips run 61 m across it. In sun they read as tar seams or unexplained shadows, and at dusk as gaps (DuskTransition_plaza_eye). From above (GoldenDay_aerial_sw, GoldenDay_valley_air), the slab's 0.3 m edge sits on the lawn like a box.
  - Likely cause: Zone1_VisitorCenterBuilder.cs line 1501 builds MainPlaza_Paving as one slab. Lines 1508-1509 add Plaza_AxisStripW/E, 0.4 x 61 m strips in grey MAT_Z1_PlazaStrip.
  - Suggested fix: Break up the field: planting islands with mature trees and under-planting, a centrepiece on the entrance axis (a fountain or a dinosaur statue/skeleton), ticket booths and kiosks, banners on the lamp posts, flagpoles, shade sails or umbrellas, way-finding signs, wear decals and moss in the joints. Replace the thin strips with a 1.5-2 m granite band with edge setts, or remove them. Hide the slab edge with a planted border.
- **[major] GoldenDay_plaza_eye.png** (geometry): The Visitor Center reads as a glass shoebox: a flat rectangular silhouette with no roofline or eaves, two big panes showing a flat-lit, empty interior, a tiny entrance canopy, and nothing planted at its base. From above (GoldenDay_aerial_sw, GoldenDay_valley_air), the two roofs beside the glass atrium are flat, untextured cream slabs, the brightest things in the frame.
  - Likely cause: The building is made of box room shells from WorldArchitectureKit. LookRoofs is supposed to cap flat roofs with bitumen, parapets and rooftop units, but these two cream roofs are not capped.
  - Suggested fix: Give the hero building a silhouette: a deep overhanging roof (the genre icon is a big timber or thatch roof), a projecting entrance canopy on columns, a stone plinth, planted beds and banners along the facade, and interior dressing visible through the glass. Find out why LookRoofs skips the cream roofs and cap them.
- **[major] GoldenDay_coast_eye.png** (geometry): The overlook rail in the ending shot is made of primitive boxes. The rails are square sections with a floor-plank texture whose seams repeat every 0.5 m or so like bricks. The posts are plain dark square sticks with no base plate or fixings. The same rail is the silhouette in DawnCoast_coast_eye, the last view of the game.
  - Likely cause: Zone4_TerrainJungleAndDawnCoastBuilder.cs lines 504-508: the rails are CreateBox 0.14 x 0.10 x 8 m with p.WoodPlanks, and the posts are 0.16 m boxes in DarkPaintedMetal.
  - Suggested fix: Model a proper timber-and-steel rail (chamfered or rounded rails, bolted brackets, steel posts with base plates and anchors) or use a rail prop from Assets/Art. Map a long-grain timber texture along the rail and add weathering, lichen and salt stains.
- **[major] GoldenDay_valley_air.png** (water): The Tyrant Valley river reads as a dry ditch: a dark trench 4-8 m wide with brown banks and a near-black bed. There is no visible water surface, sky reflection, foam or reeds. It is the main feature of the frame and does not read as water.
  - Likely cause: LookTerrainWater.BuildStream line 170 sets the water level to the trough bottom + 0.35 m, about 0.75 m below the banks (line 153 notes banks about 1.1 m above the bed). The tannin colours are very dark (scatteringColor (0.07,0.10,0.06), absorptionDistance 1.1 m). In a deep trench seen at 45 deg, the surface only reflects the dark banks.
  - Suggested fix: Raise the level to about 0.25 m below the bank tops for a wider surface. Lighten scatteringColor to about (0.10,0.20,0.16). Add a current map and foam on bends and around the bridge. Soften the banks with reeds, rocks and overhanging ferns.
- **[minor] GoldenDay_valley_eye.png** (vegetation): The tall grass beside the path stands in two blocks of uniform height with straight vertical walls along the path, like a hedge or a crop field.
  - Likely cause: LookVegetationDetails.cs line 163: the road mask is binary (blocked means no detail), so density drops from full to zero at the path edge.
  - Suggested fix: Fade density and height over 1.5-2 m from the road edge, broken up with noise. Let some blades lean over the path, and add a strip of short tufts and bare mud at the edge.
- **[minor] DawnCoast_coast_eye.png** (materials): The wet asphalt and the verge behind the rail are covered in sparkles, many of them green and cyan. It reads as glitter rather than a wet road.
  - Likely cause: Specular aliasing on the wet normal map (dawn wetness 0.5, AtmosphereController.cs line 129), with no TAA in the free camera. Chromatic aberration 0.04 (LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs line 169) then splits each sparkle into coloured dots.
  - Suggested fix: Set chromatic aberration to 0 in the day and dawn presets (keep about 0.03 for night). Clamp wet smoothness or reduce normal strength at distance. Verify with TAA in Play mode.
- **[minor] GoldenDay_aerial_sw.png** (vegetation): Two bare brown slope patches on the hills (upper middle and upper right; lit orange in DuskTransition_aerial_sw) read as landslides or dead ground on a lush island.
  - Likely cause: Probably slopes where no trees were planted, leaving a ground layer (leaf litter or rock) that reads brown in sun.
  - Suggested fix: Plant them with ferns, shrubs and vines, or paint them as dark basalt with moss so they read as cliffs, not scars.
- **[minor] GoldenDay_aerial_sw.png** (water): The Titan Meadows lake is a near-perfect oval of dark olive water inside a uniform beige sand ring. From above it reads as a decal.
  - Likely cause: The LakeShore mud/sand weight is a constant band around LakeLevel (LookTerrainLayers.cs lines 151-157). The water is a quad pool with murky colours (LookTerrainWater.BuildLake).
  - Suggested fix: Break the ring with noise and reed beds all round. Vary the shoreline (bays, a muddy beach where sauropods drink) and lighten the shallows.
- **[minor] GoldenDay_yard_eye.png** (materials): The yard perimeter wall reads as a flat beige box: one tan colour with faint panel joints, and no stains, rust streaks, moss or vines visible from 40 m.
  - Likely cause: ConcreteExterior (WorldMaterialPalette.cs line 113) is warmed by the day grade. The dressing in DressPerimeterWall is on the outer face or too subtle to survive the distance.
  - Suggested fix: Add stronger grime and rain streaks under the coping, rust stains, moss at the base and vines on the inner face, and use a cooler grey concrete.
- **[minor] GoldenDay_yard_eye.png** (vegetation): The same baobab-like tree repeats six times in a row behind the wall, at the same size and pose. Baobabs also read as African savanna rather than a rainforest island (also on the right of GoldenDay_valley_eye and GoldenDay_meadow_eye).
  - Likely cause: n/a
  - Suggested fix: Vary scale and rotation, break the row, and mix in rainforest species (ficus, kapok, tree ferns, palms).
- **[minor] DuskTransition_meadow_eye.png** (weather): The lightning bolt is a thin hairline with a tiny fork, and the cloud base around it is not lit, so it reads as an overlay.
  - Likely cause: Bolt mesh width and glow in LightningBoltController; no cloud flash is visible in this frame.
  - Suggested fix: Give the bolt a thicker core with a soft glow and more branching, and flash the cloud base around the strike in the same frame.
