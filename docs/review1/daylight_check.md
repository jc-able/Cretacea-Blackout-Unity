# Review 1 — second opinion on segment `daylight` (score 4/10)

A second, sceptical critic looked at the same captures: which findings hold (with what was measured), which do not, and what the first critic missed.

## Kept (confirmed)

- **[blocker] DuskTransition_plaza_eye.png** (vegetation (far-tree impostors)): The jungle hill behind the Visitor Center is the largest thing in the frame, and it renders as a heap of flat mustard-yellow cards. Each card is lit on one half and dark on the other, with vertical drips and no leaf detail. The same defect shows elsewhere: a khaki crumpled-paper hill in DuskTransition_meadow_eye, grey-tan stone blocks behind the mesh trees in DawnCoast_valley_eye, and tan paper cut-outs in all three aerials (worst at the right edge of DawnCoast_aerial_sw). By day, from the plaza and the meadow, the hill passes at distance; only pale trunk streaks show.
  - Fix: In LookVegetationImpostors.SaveMesh, replace the per-vertex side normals normalize(up*0.35 ± side*0.9). They split every card into a lit half and a dark half. As a minimum, give each card one shared up-and-outward normal. Better: derive a tangent-space normal map from the depth[] that Rasterise() already fills, and bind it instead of clearing _NormalMap. Use the foliage translucent material type with the near trees' diffusion profile. Darken and desaturate the baked albedo so it matches the mesh trees (the 1.12 'shade' lift in Rasterise brightens it). Bake AO from depth into the atlas, or let the impostors cast cached shadows (shadowCastingMode is Off now). For hills seen from above, move to hemi-octahedral impostors with 8-12 views, including a top view.
  - Why it is real: A zoomed crop shows uniform yellow card shapes with a hard lit/dark split and dripping verticals. Region (100-800,150-330) averages (112,104,77), P95 luminance 231. The code confirms the cause: SaveMesh writes opposite side-facing normals on the left and right vertices of every quad; SaveMaterial removes the normal and mask maps and forces MaterialId.LitStandard; shadows are off. Players see this: opening shot 5 (StoryDirector, 30-34 m up during the DuskTransition blend, looking north across the plaza to this hill), and the ending crane shot, which rises to about 15 m to look at the valley canopy.
- **[blocker] DawnCoast_coast_eye.png** (exposure / grade): The ending view is blown out. The right half of the sky is a flat lemon-yellow wash around a sun blob about 200 px wide, bloom veils the whole frame, and the sun's reflection is a clipped white bar with hard edges. The sky never turns peach, and the sea is khaki-grey instead of teal. DawnCoast_valley_eye has the same burn: 16.6% of its pixels have a clipped channel and the far trees dissolve into glare.
  - Fix: The critic's fix holds. Raise shoulderLength from 0.55 to 2.5-3.0, which by HDRP's Hable formula (W = initialW + 2^len - 1) puts the white point at about 5.7-8, then re-tune evCompensation per preset. Dawn: bloomIntensity 0.18 -> about 0.06, bloomScatter 0.68 -> 0.55, horizonTint about (1.0,0.84,0.76), zenithTint about (0.82,0.90,1.0); lower the sun from 9.5 deg to 3-4 deg; add swell so the glitter path widens. Two cautions. The shoulder lives in baseLook, so it also moves NightCalm and NightStormBlackout, which bible section 4 balances at EV 4: either give only day, dusk and dawn the long shoulder (every preset uses the Custom curve, so they still cross-fade) or re-measure the night grey card. Raising dawn evMax above 13 also means updating the bible's exposure table.
  - Why it is real: Measured: in the sky (top 335 rows), 36.6% of pixels have a clipped channel; in the right half, 68%, mean (244,231,187). Pure white is only 6.7% of the sky and 3.5% of the frame, so the critic's '21%/38% to white' is mostly yellow channel clipping, but the burn is obvious. The reflection column (840-895,360-445) is 100% clipped with straight edges. The sea (0-800,370-440) is (127,115,82). The base curve's white point computes to about 1.49 (toe 0.15/0.5, shoulder 0.75/0.55). This is the last image of the game: PlayDawnCoastCraneShot opens at this rail looking toward (900,-12,z), the same heading, and the sun (azimuth 110, elevation 9.5) sits about 20 deg right of centre, inside the 44-48 deg shot.

  - Fix: Recapture review frames with --far_clip 4000 (the player's value) or more. In the game, push the player and cutscene far clip well past 4 km, or fade the sea fully into haze before 4 km. Give each preset its own groundTint in a sea/haze colour instead of the shared olive (0.10,0.13,0.08). That colour also feeds the fog (colorMode SkyColor) and the lower half of the ambient probe.
  - Why it is real: Rows 338-362 are a flat (90,98,91), std 10-14. The strip is about 30 px, 2.5 deg at the capture's 60 deg FOV, which is exactly atan(43 m / 1000 m). The cause: `cretacea capture` defaults to farClip 1000 (CretaceaCliTools.cs line 52), while the player and cutscene cameras use 4000 (CretaceaWorldAssembler.cs lines 462/599/623, CameraDirector.cs line 126). In the game the strip shrinks to about 0.6 deg (about 7 px) but does not vanish, and the ending's sun glitter still stops at it. groundTint is set at LookAtmosphereProfiles.cs line 156.

Check against a grey card: sunlit red/blue ratio about 1.2-1.35, shade about 0.9.

## Dropped (not real, overstated, or a capture artefact)

- Horizon finding, aerial part: the khaki top third of every aerial reads as smog and gives the aerials their sepia cast
  - Why not: Mostly a capture artefact. `cretacea capture` defaults to a 1000 m far clip, so the sea is cut off and the olive ground of the physically based sky shows through. The game's cameras use 4000 m, and no player ever sees 140-220 m aerials. The coast-band part of the finding is kept.
- Day-shade finding, one example: the valley path in GoldenDay_valley_eye reads as black tar
  - Why not: The path is very dark (mean 24,22,15), but the gravel stones stay visible (P95 43), and dark gravel under a cloud shadow is plausible. The coast and yard examples carry the finding without it.
- Dawn-ending finding, the numbers: 21% of the frame and 38% of the sky clip to white
  - Why not: Pure white is 3.5% of the frame and 6.7% of the sky. The 20.8% and 36.6% figures count pixels with one or two channels clipped, which show as lemon-yellow. The finding is kept with corrected numbers.
- Dusk-exposure finding: DuskTransition_plaza_eye clips 37% of its upper-right sky
  - Why not: Measured 22% with a clipped channel and 12.6% pure white across the upper-right quadrant, and 28% in the brightest corner. The clipping is real but smaller than claimed, and the shoulder change in the ending finding covers it.
- Cloud finding: shaded cloud bases are olive, about (73,78,73)
  - Why not: The bases measure neutral to faintly green grey: (85,92,92), (103,106,103), (94,99,101), red/blue 0.93-0.99. The soft blob is real; the olive cast is not, so the finding is kept as minor.

## Missed by the first critic

- **[minor] GoldenDay_plaza_eye.png** (exposure / tone curve): Daylight whites stop short and turn cream. The brightest 0.1% of the plaza frame is 178/255 and of the coast frame 201. Sunlit cumulus tops sit at about 150-190 in cream-grey (the yard's top cloud is (153,142,127)). The median luminance of the seven GoldenDay frames is only 48-74. With the crushed toe, the day uses a narrow, dim slice of the range and looks muddy rather than bright and tropical. Cause: the shoulder spans only about 2 stops (the linear segment ends at an exposed value of 0.34 and reaches white at 1.49 with toe 0.28/0.5 and shoulder 0.75/0.55), and temperature +4 with smhHighlights (1.04,1,0.95) tints the whites cream.
  - Fix: After the shoulder change, re-measure the day evCompensation (now +0.5) so that sunlit paving of about 21% albedo reads about 100-110 sRGB and sunlit cumulus tops about 225-240, with under 1% clipping. Make the highlight grade neutral (smhHighlights 1,1,1; temperature about 0).
- **[minor] GoldenDay_meadow_eye.png** (terrain colour): Orange-brown flecks in the lawn texture make Titan Meadows look dry and autumnal. 13% of the lawn pixels are orange-brown, and the lawn averages (52,61,14), a red/blue ratio of 3.7 with almost no blue. At dusk (DuskTransition_meadow_eye) the flecks reach 23% and the whole meadow turns khaki-brown. This lawn fills the lower half of the game's first shot.
  - Fix: In derive_terrain_textures.py, remove or desaturate the orange-fleck component of tj_grass_BaseColor (keep leaf litter on TL_JungleFloor only), and shift the grass slightly toward blue-green so sunlit lawn reads at a red/blue ratio of about 2-2.5.
- **[minor] GoldenDay_coast_eye.png** (geometry (props)): The ending's 'Coast Guard rescue vessel' (DistantEvacBeacon_Bay, Zone4_TerrainJungleAndDawnCoastBuilder.cs lines 524-546) is about 15 primitive boxes, including a 32 x 9.6 m PlasterClean slab standing in for wake foam. The crane shot's final move (overlook + (3,9.5,-3), looking out to sea) is written to frame it, so the last image of the game likely shows a box ship. Only the fragment is in these captures.
  - Fix: Capture the crane shot to confirm. Replace the boxes with a boat model from Assets/Art (or a silhouette card at that distance), and use foam or a water decal for the wake instead of a plaster slab.
